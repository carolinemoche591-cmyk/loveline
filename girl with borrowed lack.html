Chapter One

Amelia Hart and the Universe's Personal Grudge

Some people have bad days. Amelia Hart had bad decades.

Amelia Hart had always believed that bad luck was something that happened occasionally, like rain in summer or finding out that the restaurant had run out of your favourite dessert, but somewhere between the age of seven and twenty-two she had gradually come to the conclusion that bad luck was not something that happened to her; bad luck was something that had apparently chosen her, rented a small apartment inside her life, unpacked several suitcases, put its feet on the table and decided it was never leaving.

She had evidence. At seven, she had fallen into a pond while trying to rescue a duck that had not needed rescuing. At nine, she had accidentally won a school competition she had never entered because her teacher had confused her name with another student's, and when she tried to explain the mistake, the microphone fell on her head. At twelve, she had been chased by a goose for nearly half a kilometre, even though she had never met the goose before and had no idea why it appeared to have such strong personal feelings about her.

At fifteen, she had gone to a birthday party wearing a beautiful cream dress, only to discover that the birthday cake had somehow been placed directly above the chair she sat on, and when the ceiling decoration fell, the cake followed it. At eighteen, she had bought a lottery ticket with her last few coins. She had won. The prize was another lottery ticket. The second ticket had won nothing. At twenty-one, she had finally decided that perhaps adulthood would bring her some dignity. It had not.

Now, at twenty-two, Amelia stood in front of her bedroom mirror on a Tuesday morning, wearing a soft blue sweater, dark trousers and one sock that was mysteriously inside out, while she tried to convince herself that today would be different.

She had long, warm brown hair that naturally curled slightly at the ends, although she usually tied it into a loose ponytail because she claimed that spending twenty minutes fighting with her hair before breakfast was not an acceptable use of her life. Her eyes were hazel, bright enough to look almost green under certain light and almost golden under others, and whenever she was amused there was a little crease near her left eye that made it obvious she was trying not to laugh.

She looked perfectly ordinary. That was the problem. There was nothing about Amelia that suggested she was the centre of a supernatural mystery. She didn't have glowing eyes. She couldn't fly. She didn't talk to ghosts. She couldn't turn invisible. She couldn't read minds. As far as Amelia knew, her only supernatural ability was attracting objects travelling at unreasonable speeds directly toward her forehead.

That morning, she stood in the kitchen holding a mug of tea. She took one sip. The handle broke. The mug fell. Amelia watched it hit the floor. It didn't break. She smiled.

Nice try.

Then the tea splashed upward and landed directly on her white shirt. Amelia stared down at herself. She slowly looked toward the ceiling.

"I would like to formally complain."

Nothing answered.

"Okay."

She wiped herself with a towel. Then she opened the refrigerator. The light didn't come on. She opened it again. Nothing. She closed it. Opened it. Still nothing.

Amelia sighed. "Of course."

She reached inside. A container of yoghurt fell out. She caught it. She smiled.

"Got you."

Then the lid opened. Yoghurt landed on her cheek.

Amelia closed her eyes. She whispered, with the exhausted patience of a woman who had already fought two battles before seven in the morning:

Today is going to be wonderful.

And somewhere, though Amelia didn't know it yet, something was already listening.

Chapter Two

The Stranger With the Coffee

Sometimes the person who changes your life arrives carrying coffee.

The first time Amelia met Daniel Vale, she was running late, carrying a handbag that had somehow become heavy enough to qualify as gym equipment, holding an umbrella that she did not need, and trying to eat a piece of toast while simultaneously tying her shoelace.

This was not a good combination. It became considerably worse when she walked directly into a lamppost. The impact wasn't serious, but it was loud enough for three people to turn around.

Amelia stood there with her eyes closed. The toast was still in her mouth. A woman walking past stared at her.

Amelia removed the toast and said, "Good morning."

The woman smiled awkwardly. "Good morning."

Amelia nodded. "Nothing happened."

The woman continued walking. "I didn't see anything."

"Thank you."

Amelia continued toward the bus stop. She arrived just as the bus doors closed. She stared. The driver stared at her through the windshield. Amelia lifted one hand. The driver shook his head.

She ran alongside the bus for three steps. The bus continued moving.

You're not serious!

The bus disappeared around the corner. Amelia stood there breathing heavily. Then someone beside her said, "You know, technically, running after it probably makes the driver less likely to stop."

Amelia turned. A young man was standing there holding a takeaway coffee.

He was tall, perhaps around six feet, with dark blond hair that looked as though he had combed it with his fingers rather than a brush. His eyes were blue-grey, calm and slightly amused, and he had the irritatingly relaxed expression of someone whose morning had probably gone exactly according to plan.

He wore a dark brown coat over a cream sweater, black trousers and worn boots. There was something effortlessly attractive about him, not because he seemed to be trying to impress anyone, but because he seemed completely unaware that he was doing it.

Amelia looked at him. Then she looked at the road. Then back at him.

"I know."

He smiled. "Just making conversation."

"I've had a difficult morning."

"I noticed."

"You saw the lamppost?"

"I saw the lamppost lose."

Amelia stared at him for a second. Then she laughed. It surprised her. She hadn't intended to laugh.

Daniel smiled.

"There. Better."

"What?"

"You were angry."

"I wasn't angry."

"You were."

"I was frustrated."

"That's angry wearing a nicer dress."

Amelia looked at him. "That's actually a very good sentence."

"I have those occasionally."

The next bus arrived. They boarded. Amelia sat beside the window. Daniel sat beside her.

She looked at him. "Do you always sit next to strangers?"

"Only interesting ones."

She raised an eyebrow. "You don't know me."

"I know you lost a fight with a lamppost."

"It was a draw."

Daniel laughed.

The bus began moving. Amelia took out her phone. She checked the time. Then the phone slipped from her hand.

She gasped.

Before it reached the floor, Daniel caught it.

He handed it back.

You're welcome.

Amelia blinked. "Thank you."

She looked at the phone. Then at him. Something felt strange. Not frightening. Just strange. She couldn't explain it.

The bus turned sharply. Someone's bag fell. A man's newspaper flew away. A child dropped a toy. Amelia's phone remained perfectly still. Her coffee cup didn't spill. Her handbag didn't fall. Nothing happened.

She stared at the peaceful situation.

Daniel noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You look suspicious."

"I am suspicious."

"Of me?"

"Maybe."

He smiled.

"Good."

Then the bus stopped. Daniel's coffee tipped. Amelia instinctively reached for it. But before she could touch it, the cup stopped in mid-air.

For half a second, neither of them moved.

The cup floated between them.

Daniel calmly caught it.

Amelia stared.

He stared at her.

She pointed at the cup.

"You did that."

"No."

"Your coffee was floating."

"Maybe it was having a good day."

"Coffee doesn't have good days."

"You don't know that."

Amelia narrowed her eyes.

"Who are you?"

He smiled.

"Daniel."

"Just Daniel?"

"For now."

Chapter Three

The Day Everything Went Right

Amelia discovered something unbelievable: she could actually have a good day.

Amelia discovered something unbelievable that afternoon. She had good luck. For approximately six hours. And those six hours were so beautiful that Amelia nearly cried.

She walked into a café. There was one empty table. She got it. She ordered her favourite cake. They had it. She paid. The exact amount came out of her wallet. No missing coins. No wrong change. No machine malfunction. Nothing.

Amelia sat down. She took a bite. The cake was perfect. She looked toward heaven.

Thank you.

Daniel arrived shortly afterward and sat across from her. "You look happy."

"I am."

"Why?"

"Nothing has gone wrong."

Daniel smiled. "Congratulations."

"I think I'm cured."

"Cured of what?"

"My life."

He laughed. Amelia continued eating.

For the next few hours, they walked around the city together. They talked about everything. Daniel told her he liked old books, terrible movies, quiet cafés and people who could laugh at themselves. Amelia told him she liked music, rainy evenings, strawberry cake, sleeping late on weekends and people who didn't take themselves too seriously.

Daniel asked her why she always carried an umbrella. She answered honestly.

Because something always falls from the sky.

He laughed. "You can't be serious."

"I am."

At that exact moment, a bird dropped something. It landed three metres away.

Amelia pointed.

"See?"

Daniel looked at the bird, then at Amelia.

"That doesn't count."

"It absolutely counts."

He laughed again.

Amelia smiled. She liked his laugh. She liked the way he listened. She liked that he never made her feel ridiculous when she described her bad luck.

And she liked something else. When she was with Daniel, things didn't go wrong.

They were walking beside a fountain when Amelia accidentally dropped her purse. Daniel caught it. She bumped into a stranger. The stranger apologized. A cyclist almost hit them. The cyclist somehow avoided them. A waiter dropped a tray. It landed perfectly on a table.

A dog ran toward Amelia.

Instead of biting her, it sat down and offered her its paw.

Amelia stared at it.

Daniel stared at it.

The dog looked between them.

Amelia shook its paw.

You're new.

The dog wagged its tail. Daniel laughed so hard he had to sit down.

For once, life felt easy. And that frightened her more than bad luck ever had.

Chapter Four

Daniel's Secret

Daniel knew something was wrong. He had known since the first moment he saw Amelia. There was a strange energy around her. He couldn't see it exactly. He couldn't touch it. But he could feel it. It was like standing near a storm that hadn't decided whether to rain.

Daniel had a secret. He had always had one. He could manipulate probability. He couldn't make impossible things happen, not exactly, but he could influence the chances of things happening.

If there was a one-percent chance that a coin would land on its edge, Daniel could make that one percent become ninety percent. If there was a ninety-percent chance that a glass would fall, he could make it five percent.

He called it Luck Bending.

He had learned to control it when he was sixteen. Before that, it had been dangerous. Once, he had accidentally made every traffic light in his neighbourhood turn green at the same time. Another time, he had won twelve raffle prizes in one afternoon and accidentally attracted the attention of a man who believed Daniel was using magic to manipulate the economy.

Daniel's ability wasn't perfect. And Amelia's presence was doing something he had never experienced before.

It was absorbing his luck.

Whenever she stood near him, his power became stronger. And her misfortune disappeared.

Daniel tested it carefully. He dropped a coin. It landed standing upright. He tossed a bottle cap. It landed perfectly on a narrow wall. He threw a paper ball toward a bin without looking. It went in.

Then Amelia walked away.

The coin rolled off the table. The bottle cap fell. The paper ball bounced off the bin and hit Daniel in the forehead.

Daniel stared at the paper.

Interesting.

He looked toward Amelia. She was walking away. Three seconds later, she slipped on absolutely nothing.

Daniel caught her.

She looked embarrassed.

"Thanks."

He smiled.

"No problem."

Inside his mind, however, one thought repeated itself.

Her bad luck isn't disappearing. It's moving.
Chapter Five

The Distance Rule

They discovered the rule by accident. Amelia was standing in Daniel's apartment kitchen while he prepared dinner. She was cutting vegetables. Daniel was cooking. Everything was peaceful.

Too peaceful.

Amelia looked at the knife, then at Daniel.

"I think something's wrong."

Daniel turned.

"What?"

She pointed at the kitchen.

"Nothing has happened."

Daniel laughed.

"That's the problem?"

"Yes."

She waited. Nothing happened. She dropped a carrot. It landed normally. She opened a drawer. It opened normally. She reached for a plate. The plate remained in her hand.

She looked at Daniel.

"This is unnatural."

Then Amelia's phone rang. She stepped outside to answer it.

The moment she crossed the doorway, a flowerpot fell from a balcony. It missed her by centimetres.

She stared at it.

What?

She took another step. A bicycle nearly hit her. She stepped backward. A delivery box fell from a doorway. She jumped sideways. The box landed where she had been standing.

Amelia slowly turned around. Daniel was standing in the doorway. He looked at the flowerpot, then the bicycle, then the box, then Amelia.

Come back inside.

She did.

The moment she entered, everything stopped.

Silence.

Daniel slowly crossed his arms.

We have a problem.

Amelia nodded.

"I know."

He looked at her.

"Your bad luck disappears when you're close to me."

Amelia blinked.

"And comes back when I'm away."

"Exactly."

She thought about it.

Then she smiled.

So... you are basically a human umbrella.

Daniel stared at her.

"What?"

"You protect me from disaster."

Daniel laughed.

"That's not what I meant."

"I like it."

"You would."

She smiled.

Then she asked:

"How far away can I be?"

Daniel didn't know.

They tested it.

One metre. Fine. Five metres. Fine. Ten metres. Fine. Twenty metres. Fine. Fifty metres. Amelia dropped her phone. One hundred metres. A bicycle chain snapped. Two hundred metres. A pigeon flew directly into a man's sandwich.

Daniel looked at Amelia.

She looked at him.

That's not my fault.

Daniel laughed.

But neither of them noticed the woman watching them from across the street.

She had been there for several minutes. She wore a long grey coat. In her hand was an old black notebook.

On the first page was written:

THE LUCK GIRL HAS BEEN FOUND.
Chapter Six

The Woman in the Grey Coat

The woman followed them for three days. She never approached. She simply watched. Daniel noticed her on the fourth day. He didn't tell Amelia immediately because Amelia had enough problems without adding "mysterious woman following us" to her growing collection of concerns.

Unfortunately, Amelia noticed her anyway.

"Daniel."

"Yes?"

"Do you know that woman?"

He looked. The woman was standing across the street.

"No."

"She's been looking at us."

"I know."

"You knew?"

"Yes."

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"I was going to."

"When?"

"Eventually."

"That's reassuring."

The woman turned and walked away.

Amelia looked at Daniel.

"Should we follow her?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because that's exactly what someone wants us to do."

Amelia nodded.

"Good point."

She paused.

"Let's follow her."

Daniel sighed.

"Of course."

They followed.

The woman entered an old bookstore.

Daniel opened the door.

A bell rang.

Inside, thousands of books covered the walls. Amelia looked around.

This place is beautiful.

Daniel nodded.

The woman was nowhere to be seen.

Then an old man appeared behind the counter. He looked at them. Then at Amelia.

His face changed.

You.

Amelia pointed at herself.

"Me?"

The old man nodded.

You're the one.

Daniel stepped forward.

"The one what?"

The old man didn't answer. Instead, he reached beneath the counter and pulled out a book. It was covered in dust.

The title was written in gold:

THE LEDGER OF FORTUNE

He placed it in front of Amelia. She opened it. Every page was blank. Until she touched it.

Words appeared.

Her name appeared first.

AMELIA HART.

Then another sentence appeared.

THE GIRL WHO CARRIES THE UNLUCKY THREAD.

Amelia swallowed.

Daniel leaned closer.

Another sentence appeared.

SHE WAS NOT BORN UNLUCKY.

Amelia stopped breathing.

Then the final words appeared.

SHE WAS MADE THAT WAY.

The bookstore became completely silent.

Chapter Seven

The Truth About Her Bad Luck

The old man explained everything slowly because, according to him, some truths were too dangerous to deliver quickly. He told Amelia that centuries ago there had been people who could influence fortune.

Some could attract luck. Some could steal it. Some could transfer it. And some could store it.

Amelia belonged to the last group.

Except she didn't know it.

Her family had been protecting a supernatural force called the Fortune Thread, an invisible energy that connected people to possibility itself.

Every person carried a tiny amount. Most people never noticed it. But certain families could manipulate it.

Amelia's ancestors had been guardians.

Until someone betrayed them.

A woman named Celeste Vane had attempted to steal the Fortune Thread for herself.

She failed.

Instead, she cursed Amelia's family. The curse transformed fortune into misfortune.

And Amelia inherited it.

Daniel listened carefully.

So you're saying Amelia's bad luck is actually stolen fortune?

The old man nodded.

"Exactly."

Amelia looked offended.

Wait.

Everyone looked at her.

She pointed at herself.

So I've been suffering for twenty-two years because somebody messed up a magical experiment?

The old man nodded.

"Yes."

She stared at him.

That is extremely rude.

Daniel covered his mouth.

The old man smiled.

"Unfortunately, yes."

Amelia crossed her arms.

I would like compensation.

Daniel laughed. The old man laughed too. Even Amelia eventually smiled.

Then the old man became serious.

"But there is another problem."

"What?"

"The curse is getting stronger."

Amelia's smile disappeared.

"Why?"

"Because someone has begun stealing luck from other people."

Daniel's expression changed.

Celeste.

The old man nodded.

She's back.
Chapter Eight

The Worst Date in History

Daniel decided that Amelia needed one normal evening. This was an admirable goal. It was also incredibly stupid.

He took her to a restaurant. He booked a table. He checked the menu. He checked the weather. He checked the chairs. He checked the floor.

Amelia watched him.

Are you inspecting the restaurant?

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I've learned."

She laughed.

Dinner began beautifully. The food was excellent. The music was soft. The lights were warm. Amelia wore a dark green dress that made her eyes look almost golden.

Daniel looked at her for a moment too long.

She noticed.

What?

"Nothing."

"You were staring."

"I was thinking."

"About?"

"You look beautiful."

Amelia smiled.

Thank you.

Daniel looked away.

Then a waiter approached.

"Would you like dessert?"

Amelia nodded.

Absolutely.

The waiter walked away.

Two minutes later, the restaurant lights went out.

Everyone screamed.

Emergency lights came on.

A waiter slipped.

A tray flew through the air.

Daniel caught it.

Amelia clapped.

Nice.

The lights came back.

The dessert arrived.

Amelia took one bite.

Her expression changed.

This is the best dessert I've ever had.

Then the chef ran out of the kitchen.

"Who ordered the chocolate cake?"

Amelia raised her hand.

The chef pointed at her.

Congratulations.

She blinked.

For what?

"You are our one-millionth customer."

The entire restaurant applauded.

Amelia stared. Daniel laughed.

She received a giant box of chocolates, an expensive bottle of wine, a free meal voucher and a photograph.

She looked at Daniel.

Why is this happening?

He smiled.

Maybe your luck is changing.

She shook her head.

Maybe I'm borrowing yours.
Chapter Nine

The First Kiss

It happened in the rain. Of course it did.

Amelia and Daniel were standing beneath an old bridge after escaping three men who had tried to steal the Fortune Ledger. Amelia was breathing heavily. Daniel was soaked.

She looked at him.

You know... this is probably the strangest relationship I've ever had.

Daniel smiled.

We're not in a relationship.

"I know."

She paused.

But you keep saving me.

"You keep getting into trouble."

"That's not my fault."

"Mostly."

She pushed him.

He laughed.

Then she became quiet.

Daniel noticed.

What?

She looked at him.

I've spent my whole life believing something was wrong with me.

He didn't interrupt.

She continued.

"I thought maybe I was careless. Maybe stupid. Maybe cursed. Maybe the universe simply didn't like me. And now I find out there was an actual reason."

Daniel stepped closer.

You were never the problem.

She looked at him.

How do you know?

"Because I've watched you. I mean I've watched how you treat people. You help everyone, even when you have nothing. You laugh when you're embarrassed. You apologize when something isn't your fault. You pretend everything is fine when you're scared."

She looked down.

Daniel gently lifted her chin.

And you're still standing.

Amelia's eyes softened.

Daniel...

He leaned closer.

For a second, neither moved.

Then Amelia whispered:

If you kiss me and something explodes, I'm blaming you.

Daniel smiled.

Fair.

He kissed her.

Nothing exploded.

No glass shattered. No car crashed. No bird attacked anyone. Nothing.

For the first time in Amelia's entire life, something beautiful happened without being followed by disaster.

She smiled against his lips.

Then somewhere in the distance, a car alarm started screaming.

Amelia pulled away.

Daniel laughed.

She pointed at him.

Don't say anything.

"I wasn't going to."

"You were."

"I was."

She smiled.

And they kissed again.

Chapter Ten

The Woman Who Steals Luck

Celeste Vane was not a monster. That was what made her frightening. She looked completely human. She had silver hair, pale skin and calm green eyes. She dressed elegantly. She spoke softly. She smiled politely.

And she could walk into a room, shake someone's hand and steal ten years of their good fortune without them ever knowing.

She didn't steal money. She stole opportunities. A promotion. A successful surgery. A safe journey. A lucky meeting. A chance encounter.

She collected them.

And every stolen piece of fortune made her stronger.

Now she wanted Amelia.

Not because Amelia was powerful. Because Amelia was the last remaining vessel capable of holding the entire Fortune Thread.

Celeste needed Amelia alive.

But she didn't need Daniel alive.

That was the problem.

Chapter Eleven

Amelia Learns to Use Her Bad Luck

Daniel taught Amelia something important. Her bad luck wasn't completely random. It followed patterns.

If she concentrated, she could influence it.

At first she accidentally caused small disasters. A spoon fell. A chair broke. A shoe came loose. A door slammed.

Then she became better.

She could make a coin land strangely. She could cause an opponent to trip. She could make a weapon jam. She could turn someone's perfect plan into absolute chaos.

Amelia discovered something wonderful.

Her bad luck could be weaponized.

She smiled.

Daniel looked worried.

That expression concerns me.

"Why?"

"You look happy."

"I am."

"That's worse."

Amelia raised her hand.

A villain's shoe suddenly flew off.

He fell.

Daniel stared.

Amelia smiled.

See?

Daniel shook his head.

That's terrifying.

Amelia smiled wider.

Thank you.
Chapter Twelve

The Lucky Bank

They discovered a hidden building beneath London. It was called the Lucky Bank.

It contained millions of bottled fortunes.

One bottle contained someone's chance of meeting the love of their life. Another contained someone's chance of becoming wealthy. Another contained someone's chance of surviving an accident.

Amelia stared at the shelves.

This is horrible.

Daniel nodded.

Yes.

"Why would anyone collect these?"

The old librarian answered:

Because people always want what someone else has.

Amelia looked at the bottles.

Then she noticed one labelled:

AMELIA HART — UNUSED FORTUNE

She froze.

Daniel saw it.

What is that?

Amelia reached toward it.

The bottle cracked.

Golden light escaped.

Every light in the building turned on.

The ground shook.

And somewhere far away, Celeste screamed.

Chapter Thirteen

The Chase

They ran. There were people chasing them. There were magical creatures chasing them. There was a man on a motorcycle chasing them. And, for reasons Amelia never understood, there was also a goat.

"Why is there a goat?"

Daniel shouted:

Don't worry about the goat!

"THE GOAT IS CHASING US!"

"KEEP RUNNING!"

The goat ran faster.

Amelia looked behind her.

I THINK IT'S ANGRY!

Daniel shouted:

YOU PROBABLY STOLE ITS LUCK!

"WHY WOULD I STEAL A GOAT'S LUCK?"

"WE DON'T HAVE TIME!"

They turned a corner. The goat hit a trash bin. The bin exploded.

Amelia screamed.

Daniel laughed.

This isn't funny!

"It absolutely is!"

They escaped through a train station. Amelia jumped onto a train. Daniel followed. The doors closed.

The goat remained on the platform.

It stared at them.

Amelia stared back.

The goat stared.

Amelia raised one finger.

Don't.

The goat bleated.

The train moved.

Daniel sat down.

I'm going to miss that goat.

Amelia looked at him.

You are deeply strange.

"I've been told."

Chapter Fourteen

The House of Impossible Things

Their journey took them to a house that existed in two places at once. From outside, it looked ordinary. Inside, it was enormous.

There were stairs that led nowhere. Doors that opened into yesterday. Windows that showed tomorrow. A kitchen where the cupboards occasionally complained.

A bedroom changed its location every morning.

Amelia loved it.

Daniel hated it.

One morning Amelia opened the refrigerator.

A tiny man inside shouted:

Close the door!

She screamed.

Daniel ran into the room.

What happened?

"The refrigerator yelled at me!"

The tiny man shouted:

It's cold!

Daniel stared.

Why is there a man in the refrigerator?

The tiny man looked offended.

Why aren't you?

Amelia laughed so hard she had to sit down.

For the first time, Daniel realized something. He had fallen in love with her.

Not because she was magical. Not because she was unlucky. Not because she was connected to the Fortune Thread.

He loved her because she could walk into an impossible house, meet a tiny angry refrigerator man and somehow make the situation seem completely normal.

Chapter Fifteen

The First Great Disaster

Celeste attacked at midnight.

The house shook.

Windows shattered.

Doors slammed.

The Fortune Thread screamed through the walls.

Amelia stood in the centre of the living room.

Daniel stood beside her.

Celeste appeared at the doorway.

Give me the Thread.

Amelia shook her head.

No.

Celeste smiled.

You don't understand what you carry.

Amelia raised her hands.

You're right.

She smiled.

But I'm learning.

The lights exploded.

The battle began.

Amelia discovered that her bad luck could affect magic itself. Spells missed. Weapons broke. Floors collapsed beneath enemies. One attacker slipped on absolutely nothing and fell into a bathtub.

Daniel stared.

How did you do that?

Amelia smiled.

I don't know.

Then she looked at Celeste.

But I think I'm getting good at this.
Chapter Sixteen

The Secret of Daniel's Power

Daniel finally admitted the truth. His luck wasn't his own. He had inherited it from his mother. She had been one of the last Luck Benders.

But unlike Amelia, Daniel could consciously control probability.

The reason Amelia's bad luck disappeared around him was because her curse and his power reacted naturally.

His fortune balanced her misfortune.

They were opposites.

Together, they created something rare.

Balance.

The old librarian called them:

The Lucky Pair.

Amelia hated the name.

Daniel loved it.

She told him it sounded like a children's cartoon. He said it could have merchandise. She said she would never wear the T-shirt. He said he would buy her one. She said she would burn it.

He smiled.

Romantic.

She laughed.

Chapter Seventeen

The Day Amelia Became Famous

Someone filmed Amelia using her powers.

The video went viral.

She accidentally became famous.

People called her The Luck Girl. The Curse Queen. The Walking Disaster.

One person called her Susan.

Amelia was furious about Susan.

Who is Susan?

Daniel shrugged.

Maybe someone thought you looked like Susan.

"I don't."

"I agree."

"Thank you."

The internet became obsessed. People sent her gifts. Someone sent her a lucky rabbit's foot. Amelia sent it back. Someone sent her a four-leaf clover. She accidentally killed it. Someone sent her a horseshoe. It fell off the wall and hit Daniel.

He blamed her.

She laughed.

Chapter Eighteen

The Worst Family Dinner

Daniel met Amelia's family.

It went badly.

Her mother liked him. Her father interrogated him. Her younger brother stole his wallet. Her grandmother asked when they were getting married.

Amelia nearly choked.

Daniel smiled.

Her grandmother nodded approvingly.

Then the dinner table collapsed.

Everyone stared.

Amelia sighed.

Welcome to the family.

Daniel laughed.

I think I'm staying.
Chapter Nineteen

The Lucky Festival

Every hundred years, people with supernatural abilities gathered for the Festival of Fortune.

There were magic competitions, flying races, probability games, strength contests and a cooking competition.

Amelia entered the cooking competition.

She burned water.

The judges stared.

Amelia stared at the pot.

I don't understand.

Daniel whispered:

You somehow managed to make water dangerous.

She shrugged.

Talent.

She accidentally won.

The judges declared her dish "conceptual."

Amelia received a gold medal.

She had no idea why.

Chapter Twenty

The Day Everything Stopped

Then Amelia woke up without bad luck.

At first she was happy.

Then she became frightened.

Nothing went wrong.

Nothing broke.

Nothing fell.

Nothing exploded.

She walked outside.

A bird landed on her shoulder.

She looked at it.

It didn't attack her.

Daniel arrived.

"Morning."

She smiled.

Morning.

He hugged her.

Nothing happened.

Then Amelia whispered:

Something's wrong.

Daniel frowned.

Why?

She looked at him.

My bad luck is gone.

His expression changed.

Because he knew what that meant.

Someone had taken it.

And if Amelia no longer carried the curse...

someone else did.

Chapter Twenty-One

The Curse Has a New Owner

They found the answer in the old book.

The curse had moved into Celeste.

For the first time, Celeste experienced the misfortune she had spent centuries stealing from others.

Her spells failed.

Her plans collapsed.

Her armies turned against her.

Her portals opened into bathrooms.

Her magical weapons exploded.

Her shoes disappeared.

She was furious.

Amelia watched from a distance.

Daniel asked:

Do you feel sorry for her?

Amelia thought about it.

No.

Daniel nodded.

Fair.
Chapter Twenty-Two

Amelia's Choice

The Fortune Thread could be destroyed.

If Amelia destroyed it, supernatural luck would disappear from the world.

No more Luck Benders. No more curses. No more stolen fortune. No more magical disasters.

But there would also be no more Daniel's power.

Amelia had to choose.

She stood beside the ancient Thread. Daniel stood behind her.

If you destroy it, I'll lose everything.

Amelia turned.

I'm sorry.

He smiled.

You don't have to apologize.

She stared at him.

Why?

"Because I would rather live an ordinary life with you than a magical life without you."

Amelia's eyes filled with tears.

Then she smiled.

You really are annoyingly good at saying things.

"I practice."

She laughed.

Then she made her decision.

Chapter Twenty-Three

The Thread

Amelia touched the Fortune Thread.

The world went silent.

Every magical person felt it. Every creature. Every hidden society. Every supernatural being.

Everything stopped.

Amelia closed her eyes.

She didn't destroy the Thread.

She changed it.

Instead of belonging to one person, it would belong to everyone.

No one could steal it. No one could own it. No one could control it completely.

Luck became balance.

Misfortune became possibility.

And Amelia's curse disappeared.

For the first time in her life, she was free.

Chapter Twenty-Four

An Ordinary Morning

Amelia woke up.

She opened her eyes.

Her phone worked.

Her toothbrush was where she left it.

Her shoes were dry.

Her breakfast didn't fall.

She walked outside.

Nothing happened.

She reached the bus stop.

The bus arrived.

She got on.

She sat down.

A man dropped his newspaper.

A child laughed.

A dog barked.

A woman smiled.

The world continued.

Amelia looked around.

She expected disaster.

None came.

Daniel sat beside her.

"How does it feel?"

She smiled.

Strange.

"Good strange?"

Very.

He took her hand.

She squeezed his fingers.

For once, Amelia didn't have to prepare for something to go wrong.

Chapter Twenty-Five

But Luck Is Funny

Three weeks later, Amelia won a holiday.

She stared at the ticket.

Daniel stared at the ticket.

Congratulations.

"Thank you."

"Where are we going?"

She looked at the destination.

Paris.

Daniel smiled.

Wonderful.

She smiled.

Then the ticket printer exploded.

They stared at it.

Amelia slowly turned toward Daniel.

He laughed.

Don't worry.

She smiled.

Maybe a little bit of bad luck survived.

"Maybe."

She took his hand.

Good.

He looked surprised.

Good?

She nodded.

I think I missed it.
Chapter Twenty-Six

The Wedding That Almost Didn't Happen

Years later, Daniel proposed.

He planned everything perfectly. The restaurant. The flowers. The music. The ring. The speech.

Everything.

Except the weather.

A storm arrived.

The restaurant lost power.

The flowers were delivered to the wrong address.

The musician broke his guitar string.

The cake fell over.

Daniel stood in the middle of the chaos.

Amelia laughed.

He looked at her.

You're enjoying this.

She nodded.

Very much.

He took her hand.

Will you marry me?

She smiled.

Yes.

Thunder exploded outside.

The lights returned.

The musician's guitar suddenly worked.

The cake somehow stood upright.

Daniel stared.

Amelia laughed.

See?

"What?"

She smiled.

You still have some luck.

He smiled.

Apparently.
Chapter Twenty-Seven

Amelia Hart, Lucky at Last

Amelia eventually became a writer.

Naturally, she wrote about everything that happened.

She changed some names. Changed some details. Changed the goat completely.

Daniel complained.

The goat was important.

"The goat was insane."

The goat had character.

"The goat tried to kill me."

It was misunderstood.

Amelia laughed.

She wrote for hours. Sometimes Daniel would sit beside her. Sometimes they would drink coffee. Sometimes they would argue about fictional characters.

Sometimes Amelia would stare at the page for an hour without writing a word.

Daniel would ask:

Writer's block?

She would nod.

Then he would say:

Want me to throw a shoe at you?

She would laugh.

And suddenly the words would return.

Chapter Twenty-Eight

The Last Letter

Years later, Amelia received a letter.

It was written in silver ink.

There was no address. No stamp. No name.

Only one sentence:

THE FORTUNE THREAD REMEMBERS YOU.

Amelia read it twice.

Then she looked at Daniel.

Do you think it's starting again?

Daniel took the letter.

He read it.

He looked at her.

I don't know.

She smiled.

Good.

He raised an eyebrow.

Good?

She nodded.

Because if everything were finally normal, we'd probably get bored.

Daniel laughed.

That's the woman I married.

She leaned against him.

And you love me.

"Unfortunately."

She punched his arm.

He laughed.

Chapter Twenty-Nine

The Girl Who Changed Her Luck

Amelia eventually understood something. She had spent most of her life believing that luck determined everything.

She believed good luck meant happiness. She believed bad luck meant failure. She believed unlucky people were somehow less deserving of good things.

But she had learned something more important.

Luck was only possibility.

It wasn't destiny.

A person could lose everything and still choose to stand. A person could have everything and still be miserable. A person could have terrible luck and still make somebody laugh.

A person could fall a hundred times and still get up.

Amelia had fallen more than anyone she knew.

She had broken phones. Destroyed shoes. Lost umbrellas. Been attacked by birds. Fought a magical goat. Accidentally won competitions. Burned water. Nearly destroyed a supernatural bank.

And somehow...

she had survived all of it.

She hadn't become strong because her bad luck disappeared.

She had become strong because she learned how to laugh while it was happening.

Chapter Thirty

One Last Piece of Bad Luck

On the morning of her thirtieth birthday, Amelia woke early.

Daniel was asleep beside her. Sunlight entered through the curtains. The house was quiet.

Everything was peaceful.

Amelia smiled.

She got out of bed.

She walked into the kitchen.

She made coffee.

She sat beside the window.

For several minutes, nothing happened.

Then she noticed something on the table.

A small black envelope.

She opened it.

Inside was a photograph.

It showed Amelia as a little girl. She was standing beside an old woman. Behind them was a strange golden light.

On the back of the photograph were four words:

THIS WAS NEVER OVER.

Amelia stared at it.

Daniel walked into the kitchen.

He saw her expression.

What happened?

She handed him the photograph.

He read the message.

Neither spoke.

Then Amelia slowly smiled.

Daniel looked at her.

Why are you smiling?

She looked at the photograph.

Then at him.

Because... I think my bad luck just came back.

Daniel sighed.

Amelia.

She laughed.

"What?"

"I thought we were done."

"So did I."

He sat beside her.

For a moment, they looked at the photograph together.

Then the lights flickered.

Once.

Twice.

The coffee machine switched itself on.

A spoon rolled across the table.

The curtains moved despite the closed window.

Daniel slowly looked at Amelia.

Amelia slowly looked at Daniel.

A tiny golden spark appeared above the photograph.

It floated upward.

Then another appeared.

Then another.

Hundreds of tiny lights filled the kitchen.

Amelia stared.

Daniel stood.

The lights formed words in the air.

THE THREAD HAS CHOSEN AGAIN.

Amelia swallowed.

Daniel?

"Yes?"

Do you think this is going to be a problem?

He looked at the glowing message. Then at Amelia. Then at the coffee machine, which suddenly began making coffee by itself.

He sighed.

Probably.

Amelia smiled.

Good.

Daniel laughed.

She took his hand.

Outside, the morning sun rose over the city.

Somewhere far away, something ancient opened its eyes.

Somewhere beneath the streets, a locked door unlocked itself.

Somewhere in the sky, a golden line appeared between the clouds.

And somewhere in a little kitchen, Amelia Hart—the girl who had spent her entire life being unlucky—laughed.

Because after everything she had survived, she had finally learned the greatest secret of all:

Sometimes the universe doesn't give you good luck. Sometimes it gives you someone worth being unlucky with.

And that was how Amelia's second adventure began.

Not with an explosion.

Not with a prophecy.

Not with a monster.

But with Daniel looking at the mysterious golden lights and saying:

Please tell me you didn't touch anything.

Amelia looked at him innocently.

I touched the envelope.

"Anything else?"

She thought about it.

No.

A loud crash came from upstairs.

They both looked toward the ceiling.

Amelia slowly raised her hand.

Okay... I may have touched the envelope twice.

He closed his eyes.

Of course you did.

Amelia smiled.

Come on.

"Where?"

She grabbed her coat.

Let's find out what happens.

Daniel looked at her.

Then smiled.

After you.

Amelia opened the front door.

A flowerpot fell from the balcony above them.

She moved aside.

It missed her completely.

She stared at it.

Daniel stared at it.

Amelia grinned.

See?

Daniel laughed.

You're getting better.

And together they walked into the morning.

Not knowing that somewhere beyond the clouds, something was watching them.

Something that had been waiting for Amelia for thirty years.

Something that knew the truth about her family.

Something that knew why the Fortune Thread had chosen her.

And something that had one final secret to reveal.

Amelia Hart had never actually been unlucky.

She had been protected.

And whatever had been protecting her...

had finally gone silent.
