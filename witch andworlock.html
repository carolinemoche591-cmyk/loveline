THE WARLOCK AND THE WITCH

Chapter One: The Night the Dead Came Knocking

A supernatural thriller filled with dark magic, terrible decisions, suspicious cats, and two friends who should never be allowed to investigate anything together.

1. Welcome to Blackthorn

The first rule of Blackthorn Village was simple: never open your door after midnight.

The second rule was even more important: if a dead person knocked on your door, do not invite them inside, even if they claimed to have brought money, food, or an apology for something they had done while alive.

Unfortunately, Dorian Blackwood had never been particularly good at following rules.

Dorian was a warlock of twenty-eight years, standing six feet three inches tall, with messy black hair, sharp cheekbones, mysterious silver-gray eyes, and a face that made strangers assume he was intelligent.

They were usually correct about the intelligence. The wisdom, however, was a completely different matter.

He wore a long black coat with silver buttons, a dark shirt, fitted trousers, and leather boots. A collection of enchanted rings covered his fingers, each carrying a different spell. One ring could summon fire. Another could freeze water. A third could make its wearer invisible.

The fourth ring, which Dorian had purchased from a suspicious merchant, made him smell like boiled cabbage whenever he lied.

He had discovered that unfortunate feature during a date. He had told a woman that he was a wealthy prince. She had opened the carriage window and thrown him into a bush.

Dorian lived in an enormous abandoned mansion on the northern edge of Blackthorn Forest. The house had twelve bedrooms, seven bathrooms, three secret passages, and one staircase that occasionally led to a different floor than the one he had intended to visit.

The roof leaked whenever it rained. The portraits argued among themselves. The kitchen cupboard sometimes produced live frogs instead of cups.

Dorian considered the mansion charming.

His only friend considered it a health hazard.

2. The Witch Who Hated Mondays

Her name was Seraphina Wren, although most villagers called her the Witch of the Hollow.

Seraphina was twenty-seven, with long auburn hair that curled around her shoulders, luminous green eyes, pale skin dusted with freckles, and a small scar beneath her chin from an argument with a particularly aggressive enchanted teapot.

She dressed in dark purple gowns, black boots, and a traveling cloak embroidered with tiny golden moons. Her pointed hat was decorated with feathers, charms, and a little bell that rang whenever someone nearby told a lie.

The bell rang constantly whenever Dorian visited.

Seraphina lived in a crooked stone cottage hidden beneath enormous trees. Mushrooms glowed along the garden path. Flowers whispered secrets to passing birds. A pond behind the cottage contained three enchanted frogs who claimed they had once been kings.

One of the frogs was almost certainly lying.

Her most treasured possession was a battered spell book inherited from her grandmother. Her least treasured possession was Dorian's friendship, which she described as a curse that had somehow learned to walk, talk, and borrow money.

They had been friends for eleven years.

In that time, they had accidentally summoned a demon during a cooking competition, turned the village mayor into a chicken for three days, and spent an entire afternoon trying to remove a curse from a pair of trousers.

They were not lovers. They were not secretly in love. They were simply two magical disasters who had somehow survived each other.

"If I ever die," Seraphina had once told him, "I want you to stay away from my funeral."

"Why?"

"Because you would probably raise me from the dead just to ask for the money I owe you."

Dorian had considered that a reasonable possibility.

3. The Thing in the Soup

On a stormy Thursday evening, Seraphina was attempting to prepare a perfectly ordinary dinner.

Unfortunately, ordinary dinners had never been her greatest strength.

She stood before a bubbling iron pot, stirring a mixture of mushrooms, garlic, moonwater, and something that looked suspiciously like a human fingernail.

"One pinch of powdered mandrake," she muttered. "Three drops of dragon's blood. A little salt."

The soup turned bright blue.

Seraphina frowned.

"That cannot be right."

A tiny eyeball rose to the surface.

It looked directly at her.

"You forgot the pepper," it complained.

Seraphina dropped the spoon.

The eyeball sank beneath the soup.

She stared into the pot for several seconds.

"I am never buying ingredients from that market again."

A knock sounded at her front door.

Three slow knocks.

Seraphina glanced at the clock.

Eleven fifty-nine.

Nobody visited the Witch of the Hollow at midnight. Not unless they had lost their minds, their courage, or a very expensive chicken.

"Who is it?" she called.

A sweet, elderly voice answered.

"Your grandmother, darling."

Seraphina went still.

Her grandmother had been dead for thirty years.

"My grandmother is dead."

"I know, dear. It's terribly inconvenient."

Seraphina reached for her wand.

"What do you want?"

"I have come to collect what belongs to me."

The voice no longer sounded elderly. It had become deep, hollow, and wet, as though someone were speaking through a mouth filled with graveyard soil.

Every candle in the cottage went out.

The flowers outside the window bent toward the house.

Then the voice whispered something that made Seraphina's blood turn cold.

"The warlock has already been marked."

A terrible scratching began beneath the floorboards.

Something was moving around in her cellar.

4. A Very Unfortunate Visitor

Meanwhile, Dorian Blackwood was busy losing an argument with his fireplace.

"I said burn the wood, not the furniture!"

The flames crackled indignantly.

A chair caught fire.

Dorian stared at it.

"That was my favorite chair."

The fireplace spat out a burning cushion.

"Fine. We are no longer friends."

He waved his wand and extinguished the flames. Then he sat on the floor, opened a packet of biscuits, and continued reading an ancient book about forbidden magic.

He had reached a chapter entitled Seven Ways to Avoid Being Eaten by the Dead when someone knocked at the front door.

Dorian sighed.

"If this is a tax collector, I am not home."

A voice answered from outside.

"Dorian."

He recognized the voice immediately.

It belonged to Master Aldric, his former magical mentor, who had died in a fire twelve years earlier.

Dorian slowly closed his book.

"No."

"Dorian, open the door."

"Absolutely not."

"I have important news."

"Send a letter."

"I am dead."

"Then postage should be affordable."

Silence followed.

Then the dead man laughed.

The sound echoed through the mansion, rattling the windows and shaking dust from the ceiling.

Dorian stood, his humor disappearing.

"Who are you?"

"Someone who remembers what your father did."

Dorian's fingers tightened around his wand.

He had never known his father. His mother had refused to speak about him, and every magical record mentioning the Blackwood family seemed to end with the same mysterious sentence:

The last heir must never be awakened.

The door shuddered.

A long, pale finger slipped through the keyhole.

Dorian stepped back.

"I am warning you," he said. "I am armed with powerful magic, excellent reflexes, and absolutely no patience."

The finger curled.

Something on the other side began to laugh.

Then the door exploded inward.

A tall figure stood in the doorway, dressed in a burnt funeral coat. Its face was covered by a cracked porcelain mask, and black smoke curled from beneath its collar.

Dorian raised his wand.

"Leave my house."

The creature tilted its head.

"You have your father's eyes."

Dorian fired a bolt of blue lightning.

The creature caught it between two fingers.

Then it swallowed the lightning.

It burped.

Dorian stared.

"That was extremely rude."

The creature lunged.

Dorian shouted the first spell that came into his mind.

"TRANSFORMUS CHICKENUS!"

A flash of golden light filled the hallway.

When it faded, a white chicken stood on the carpet.

It wore the creature's cracked porcelain mask.

Dorian stared at the chicken.

The chicken stared at Dorian.

Then it opened its beak and spoke in a deep, furious voice.

"You cannot escape your destiny."

Dorian pointed his wand at it.

"I can certainly escape you. I have a garden."

The chicken flapped its wings.

"THE GATE HAS OPENED!"

Dorian blinked.

"What gate?"

The chicken exploded into a cloud of black feathers.

One feather landed on Dorian's nose.

He sneezed.

Every window in the mansion shattered.

Dorian stood amid the broken glass.

"I hate Thursdays."

5. Two Friends, One Terrible Discovery

Dorian reached Seraphina's cottage twenty minutes later, carrying a lantern, his wand, and a bag of biscuits.

The forest was unusually quiet. The branches twisted overhead like the fingers of enormous sleeping giants. Pale blue lights floated between the trees, and strange symbols glimmered on the bark.

At the cottage gate, a small stone gargoyle cleared its throat.

"Password."

Dorian looked at it.

"I am her best friend."

"Incorrect."

"I am a powerful warlock."

"Irrelevant."

Dorian sighed.

"She owes me twelve silver coins."

The gate swung open immediately.

Dorian nodded approvingly.

"At last, a security system with common sense."

He entered the cottage to find Seraphina standing in the kitchen with her wand raised and a frying pan held defensively in her other hand.

"Why are you carrying a frying pan?" he asked.

"Why are you carrying biscuits?"

"Emotional support."

"Mine is a weapon."

"Mine can become a weapon if necessary."

Seraphina stared at the bag.

"Those are chocolate biscuits."

"And?"

"Give me one."

He handed her the entire bag.

She immediately ate two.

Then she told him about the voice at her door, the scratching in the cellar, and the warning about the warlock.

Dorian told her about his visitor, the lightning-eating creature, and the chicken.

Seraphina stared at him for a long moment.

"You turned an ancient evil into poultry?"

"Temporarily."

"You could have destroyed the entire village."

"But I didn't."

"You are impossible."

"And yet you invited me inside."

"I am beginning to regret it."

Before Dorian could reply, the cellar door shook violently.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Both friends fell silent.

Seraphina whispered an ancient protective spell. Dorian drew a glowing circle in the air.

The cellar door slowly opened by itself.

Beyond it lay a staircase descending into absolute darkness.

At the bottom, something began to sing.

It was an old nursery rhyme, one Seraphina remembered from her childhood.

But the words were wrong.

Instead of singing about a little lamb, the voice sang about a little witch.

Instead of singing about a shepherd, it sang about a warlock.

And when the final verse came, the voice whispered both their names.

Seraphina's face lost all its color.

"Dorian..."

"Yes?"

"That song was written by my grandmother."

He swallowed.

"Was she a talented songwriter?"

"She wrote it the night before she died."

A pale hand appeared at the bottom of the stairs.

Then another.

A figure slowly climbed toward them, dragging chains that scraped against the stone.

Dorian raised his wand.

Seraphina gripped her frying pan.

The figure stopped halfway up the staircase.

Its face was hidden behind a veil of black hair.

Then it lifted its head.

Beneath the hair was a familiar face.

A face that should have been buried for thirty years.

Seraphina dropped her wand.

"Grandmother?"

The dead woman smiled.

"Hello, my darling."

Dorian leaned toward Seraphina and whispered, "Does your family usually visit through cellars?"

She elbowed him hard in the ribs.

The old woman began to laugh.

The walls trembled. The lights went out. Every window in the cottage filled with black smoke.

When the lights returned, the woman was standing directly in front of them.

She smelled of wet soil, old roses, and something that had been buried alive.

She reached for Seraphina's face.

"You should never have become a witch."

Seraphina stepped back.

"You taught me magic."

"I taught you to keep the door closed."

The dead woman's eyes turned completely black.

"And now you have opened it."

Dorian felt something burn against his chest.

He tore open his shirt collar and discovered a symbol glowing beneath his skin: a black circle surrounded by seven silver stars.

The dead woman looked at it and bowed.

"The heir has awakened."

Dorian stumbled backward.

"What heir?"

The old woman smiled at him.

"The heir to the throne beneath the world."

Then she turned toward Seraphina.

"And you, my darling, are the key that will unlock it."

The cottage shook violently.

Somewhere far beneath Blackthorn Village, a gigantic lock clicked open.

A sound like a thousand dead voices rose from the earth.

The old woman vanished.

Silence followed.

Dorian slowly buttoned his shirt.

Seraphina picked up her wand with trembling fingers.

They looked at each other.

"We are going to need a bigger frying pan," Dorian said.

Seraphina stared at him.

"That is your contribution to this situation?"

"I am thinking strategically."

She took a deep breath.

"We need to find out what your family has to do with the Gate of Hollowmere."

"Agreed."

"We need to investigate the ruins beneath the village."

"Agreed."

"And we need to do it before whatever is down there escapes."

Dorian picked up the bag of biscuits.

"Agreed. But first, breakfast."

A tremendous crash sounded beneath the cottage.

The floor split open.

From the darkness below came a voice that was neither human nor dead.

"AT LAST..."

The voice laughed.

"THE WARLOCK AND THE WITCH ARE TOGETHER."

Dorian stared into the darkness.

Seraphina raised her wand.

And somewhere in the forest, every dead tree began to grow leaves.

Blackthorn Village had seven days left.

Neither of them knew it yet.

END OF CHAPTER ONE
