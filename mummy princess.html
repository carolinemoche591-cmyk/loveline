Prologue

The Princess Who Refused to Die

Three thousand years before the first person complained about slow internet, the kingdom of Khemet flourished beneath the golden sun of ancient Egypt.

The Nile ran through its heart like a ribbon of liquid silver. Fields of wheat stretched toward the horizon. White sails drifted across the water, and enormous temples rose above the city.

At the center of this kingdom stood the royal palace of Pharaoh Amenhotep the Third. And in that palace lived Princess Nefertari Amunet.

She was twenty-one years old, the only surviving daughter of the pharaoh, and the most talked-about woman in the kingdom.

She was intelligent, proud, beautiful, stubborn, and feared by foolish men. She could read ancient languages, identify medicinal plants, calculate the stars, and negotiate treaties while criticizing a man's sandals.

"I am not marrying a man simply because his father owns a large army," she told the pharaoh.

"What if he owns two large armies?" her father asked.

"Then he has twice as many soldiers and the same small mind."

But a powerful priest named Kheper was preparing to destroy everything she loved. He had discovered an ancient power hidden beneath the royal tombs: the Heart of the First Sun.

On the night the stars disappeared behind a black storm, Kheper entered the royal sanctuary carrying a golden dagger. The curse began when he broke the seal protecting the boundary between life and death.

Nefertari tried to stop him. Their hands touched the sacred jewel together, and white light exploded through the chamber.

When the light faded, the princess lay inside a golden sarcophagus, preserved by a curse that would imprison her for three thousand years.

The door has been opened. The dead will walk again.

She slept while her palace became sand, her kingdom became history, and generations forgot the sound of her father's voice.

Then, on an ordinary Tuesday morning, a young man named Daniel Brooks made a very bad decision. He went looking for a tomb.

Chapter One

The Man Who Should Have Stayed in Bed

Daniel Brooks was twenty-seven years old, six feet tall, and possessed the kind of face that made strangers trust him before he had done anything to deserve their confidence.

He had warm brown skin, dark eyes, untidy black curls, and a smile that appeared whenever he was nervous. His faded jeans and old denim jacket had survived two breakups and one unfortunate encounter with a washing machine.

He was an amateur documentary photographer who dreamed of making films about forgotten civilizations. He was not a trained archaeologist, had no formal permission to enter the tomb, and had a flashlight that was already blinking.

"If anything happens to me," he whispered, "please tell my mother I was brave."

Then he paused.

"No. Tell her I was pushed. She'll believe that."

Inside the tomb, he discovered faded paintings, strange inscriptions, and a magnificent golden sarcophagus. He had just begun taking photographs when the floor trembled.

A golden hand emerged from beneath the lid. Then another.

A woman sat upright inside the sarcophagus. Her eyes glowed brilliant blue.

Daniel screamed, fell backward into a stone pedestal, knocked over a funerary jar, and landed on his back.

"Slave," the woman commanded.

Daniel did not move.

"Wake up!"

He opened one eye and stared at the woman leaning over him.

"You're dead!"

"I beg your pardon?"

"I saw you in the box!"

"That is a sarcophagus."

"THAT IS NOT BETTER!"

She announced herself as Princess Nefertari Amunet, daughter of Pharaoh Amenhotep and rightful heir to the throne of Khemet.

Daniel tried to explain that her kingdom was gone. When he showed her the date on his phone, she stared at the glowing screen in disbelief.

"What sorcery is that?"

"It's a phone."

"Is Samsung the name of the spirit?"

Daniel had a terrible feeling that his life was about to become very complicated.

Chapter Two

Three Thousand Years Is a Long Nap

Getting out of the tomb took forty minutes. Not because the passage was difficult, but because Princess Nefertari refused to believe the world outside had changed.

"The city is beyond those hills," she insisted.

"That city is now mostly sand," Daniel replied.

"Then we shall command the workers to rebuild it."

"The workers are not available."

"Why?"

"Because they're dead."

When she saw a plane cross the sky, she demanded to know what it ate. When she saw cars on the road, she called them chariots without horses.

"Your civilization has invented flying machines, metal chariots, and glowing communication stones, yet you have only just discovered comfortable footwear?"

Daniel had no answer.

By the time they reached the road, he had already established one important rule: Nefertari could ask five questions in a row, but after that he was allowed to drink water.

She considered the arrangement unfair.

Daniel considered it a miracle that she had agreed to it at all.

Chapter Three

The First Rule of Living with a Mummy

Daniel took Nefertari to his apartment in Cairo. She inspected the walls, curtains, kitchen, and refrigerator with the expression of a queen inspecting a poorly managed kingdom.

"You live like a peasant."

"I live like someone who pays rent."

The shower was her next enemy. When Daniel turned it on, she jumped backward and demanded that he stop the rain coming from the ceiling.

Later, Daniel's friend Marcus arrived and discovered that the mummy was real, could speak, and was currently wearing Daniel's spare T-shirt.

"Daniel, you brought a three-thousand-year-old woman into your apartment!"

"I didn't exactly plan it."

"You never plan anything!"

Nefertari folded her arms.

"I wish to know what happened to my father. I wish to know what happened to my people. And I wish to understand this world."

Daniel promised to help her.

He had no idea how many strange lessons that promise would require.

Chapter Four

The Princess Discovers Television

The following morning, Daniel woke to a scream. Nefertari was standing before the television, holding a pillow like a shield.

"THE BOX HAS CAPTURED A MAN!"

Daniel stared at the screen, where a news presenter was discussing traffic.

"It's television!"

"That is a name, not an explanation!"

Daniel explained broadcasting, cameras, sound, and the fact that the presenter was not imprisoned inside the television.

Nefertari listened carefully.

"Three thousand years, and the same fools are still in charge."

Daniel dropped onto the sofa.

"Welcome to the modern world."

Chapter Five

A Royal Visit to the Supermarket

Daniel believed a supermarket would be a safe place to introduce Nefertari to modern shopping. He was wrong.

She examined apples with price stickers, questioned why chicken was wrapped in paper, and declared that marketing was a strange and powerful god.

Her most expensive discovery was a stuffed crocodile resembling Sobek, the ancient crocodile god.

"You have spent more money on Sobek than I have spent on food this entire week," Daniel complained.

"Then Sobek is clearly more valuable than your food."

Daniel paid the bill.

He was beginning to understand that royal authority could be defeated by very few things, and a supermarket receipt was apparently one of them.

Chapter Six

The Princess Goes Viral

Daniel filmed a short video introducing Nefertari to modern objects. She identified a remote control as a device for controlling servants from a distance, and described a toothbrush as a tiny broom for bones.

Then she addressed the camera.

"Why do you take photographs of your food before eating it? Does the food taste better after you have shown it to strangers?"

Daniel posted the video. By morning, it had millions of views.

Nefertari negotiated for control over her image and demanded a share of any money earned from it. At present, she accepted payment in chocolate.

Chapter Seven

The Night the Princess Cried

That evening, Nefertari discovered that history remembered her father but knew almost nothing about his final days.

She remembered his laughter, the garden where they sat together, and the songs he sang badly when he was happy.

"They know the crown. They know the tomb. They know the gold. But they do not know that my father sang badly when he was happy."

Daniel sat beside her and told her about his grandfather, whom he had lost before he could ask all the questions he wanted to ask.

For the first time since waking, Nefertari allowed herself to cry without pretending she was a queen.

Chapter Eight

An Ancient Queen Learns to Cook

Nefertari announced that Daniel's cooking was an insult to civilization. She took over the kitchen and produced a dish involving rice, chicken, spices, and a mysterious quantity of honey.

"How much cumin did you add?" Daniel asked.

"Enough."

"How much is enough?"

"Until the ancestors approve."

The meal was surprisingly delicious. Daniel finished two bowls and asked her to teach him. She agreed, provided he taught her how to use the internet and repaired the shower.

She was still angry about the shower.

Chapter Nine

The Man in the Black Suit

Dr. Elias Voss, a respected archaeologist with a dangerous secret, watched Daniel's viral video. He recognized the blue scarab on Nefertari's hand.

Voss had spent twenty-five years searching for the Heart of the First Sun. If the princess had returned, the ancient power might still exist.

"Welcome back, Your Highness."

In a black box on his desk, a fragment of red stone began to glow.

Chapter Ten

The Princess Goes on a Date

At a café near the river, Nefertari asked Daniel whether he was married and why he had not found the right person.

She told him he was kind, somewhat foolish, and possessed a pleasant face.

"Your face is changing color," she observed.

"It's the coffee," Daniel replied.

"The coffee is in your hand."

"It's very powerful coffee."

They laughed together. When they left, she slipped her arm through his, and neither of them understood why the gesture felt so natural.

Chapter Eleven

Three Rules for a Princess

Nefertari learned about modern life while Daniel helped her build a digital archive of her lost kingdom. She wrote about the palace gardens, the royal kitchens, her father, and the ordinary people history had forgotten.

"They remember the monuments, Daniel. But I want them to remember the people."

Their website attracted historians, universities, museums, and journalists. The world debated whether she was real, a fraud, or an alien.

Nefertari had a simple response: anyone claiming she came from the moon would be personally corrected.

Chapter Twelve

The Curse Begins to Wake

Daniel noticed a dark figure appearing in photographs of the tomb. Nefertari recognized the figure as Kheper, the priest who had cursed her kingdom.

That night, the electricity failed. A cold wind moved through the apartment, and a whisper rose from beneath the floor.

The princess has returned.

Nefertari's golden scarab began to glow. Across the city, lights flickered, and from deep beneath the earth came the sound of a thousand stone doors opening.

The princess looked at Daniel.

"The curse has found me."

Then the dead began to wake.
