Chapter One

The Alpha Who Feared Love

The moon had never looked so beautiful.

Silver light spilled across the ancient forest, slipping between towering pine trees and painting the ground in shimmering patches. The night air smelled of rain, wildflowers, and something older than humanity itself. Somewhere in the darkness, a wolf howled.

Then another answered. And another. Until the entire forest seemed to be singing to the moon.

Deep within the woods stood Blackthorn Pack territory, a kingdom hidden from human eyes. Its borders were guarded by wolves who could shift between human and beast, whose strength could shatter bones, whose speed could outrun a speeding car, and whose loyalty to their Alpha was stronger than blood.

At the heart of that kingdom stood a magnificent mansion built from black stone, with enormous windows, silver gates, and balconies overlooking a valley swallowed by mist.

It belonged to one man.

Alpha Kael Draven.

Twenty-eight years old. Six feet four inches of pure trouble. Broad shoulders, a sharp jawline, raven-black hair that fell carelessly across his forehead, and eyes so pale silver that looking into them felt like staring directly into a winter storm.

Women had written poems about him. Others had cried over him. A few had actually fainted in his presence.

Kael had reacted to all three situations in exactly the same way. He had ignored them.

Because Kael Draven did not believe in love.

Love was a weakness. A distraction. A foolish little emotion that made otherwise intelligent people behave like complete idiots.

And the Alpha of Blackthorn Pack had absolutely no intention of becoming an idiot.

Unfortunately for him, fate had a wicked sense of humor.

And fate was about to introduce him to a five-foot-four human girl who would turn his perfectly organized life into a beautiful, chaotic disaster.

Her name was Amara Bennett.

And she was currently committing what might have been the stupidest act of her entire life.

She was stealing from a werewolf.

Not just any werewolf. The Alpha.

And not just stealing from the Alpha. She was stealing his underwear.

Well. Technically, she had stolen a black T-shirt, two apples, and a pair of socks.

The underwear had been an accident.

Mostly.

But before we judge her, let us return to the beginning.

Because every great love story begins with a little madness.

And this one began with a very angry wolf.

1. The Girl Who Had Absolutely No Survival Instinct

"Amara, I am going to kill you."

Amara Bennett froze.

She stood in the middle of a dark forest, clutching a paper bag against her chest while a pair of glowing yellow eyes stared at her from behind a bush.

She swallowed.

"Okay," she whispered. "First of all, that's a very aggressive way to greet someone."

The eyes narrowed.

"Second of all, if you're going to eat me, could you please wait until I've finished my apple?"

A low growl vibrated through the bushes.

Amara looked down at the half-eaten fruit in her hand. Then she looked at the eyes. Then back at the apple.

"Actually, never mind. It's a little bruised. You can have it."

She tossed the apple toward the bushes.

Something enormous moved in the darkness.

Amara screamed.

The creature emerged.

It was a wolf. No. Calling it a wolf was like calling a mountain a small pile of rocks.

This thing was enormous, with black fur that gleamed like polished obsidian, muscular legs, and teeth that looked perfectly capable of removing someone's entire arm without disturbing its dinner.

Its eyes were golden.

Its growl shook the leaves.

Amara's brain supplied one extremely useful thought.

Oh, my goodness. I am about to become a missing-person documentary.

She slowly raised both hands.

"Listen, big guy. I don't know what your problem is, but I promise I taste terrible."

The wolf tilted its head.

"I eat vegetables. Mostly. Sometimes. When my aunt is watching."

It took one step forward. Amara took three steps backward.

"Okay, you're not buying it. Fair enough."

The wolf's lips pulled back, revealing its teeth.

She squeezed her eyes shut.

"Dear God, I know we haven't talked in a while, but if You get me out of this, I promise I'll stop insulting my neighbor's cooking."

A branch snapped behind her.

Amara spun around.

Another wolf stood between the trees. This one was gray, with blue eyes and a suspiciously judgmental expression.

She stared at it.

The gray wolf stared back.

"Two of you?" she whispered. "Oh, that's just greedy."

The black wolf growled again.

"Enough!"

The voice thundered through the forest.

Both wolves immediately lowered their heads.

Amara turned.

A man stepped out of the shadows.

And for one completely unreasonable second, she forgot that she was probably about to die.

He was breathtaking.

Not in the ordinary way handsome men were breathtaking, with charming smiles and nice shoulders.

No. This man looked like somebody had taken every dangerous, beautiful thing in the world and assembled them into one deeply unfair human being.

Black hair. Silver eyes. A face carved from arrogance and expensive decisions. A black shirt stretched across a chest that made Amara briefly wonder whether the universe had a personal grudge against women with functioning eyesight.

His expression could have frozen boiling water.

He looked at her.

She looked at him.

He glanced at the paper bag in her arms. Then at the socks sticking out of it. Then at the black T-shirt.

His eyes narrowed.

"Explain."

Amara blinked.

"Explain what?"

His jaw tightened.

"Why are you on my land?"

She glanced around the forest.

"Your land?"

"Yes."

"All of it?"

His stare became colder.

She slowly lowered the bag.

"That's actually a little excessive. Do you own the trees too?"

One of the wolves made a strange choking sound.

The man turned his head slightly. The wolf immediately looked at the ground.

Amara noticed.

Interesting. The animals were afraid of him.

That was probably not a good sign.

The man stepped closer.

"Your name."

"Amara."

"Your full name."

"Amara Bennett."

"Why are you here, Amara Bennett?"

She took a breath.

"Because I got lost."

His eyes moved to the bag again.

"And the clothes?"

Amara looked down.

"Oh."

She lifted the black T-shirt.

"I found them."

"Where?"

"On a chair."

"Inside my house?"

She froze.

A long silence followed.

Then she smiled. It was a terrible smile. The kind people used when they had made a mistake so enormous that lying felt like the only reasonable option.

"Now, when you say your house..."

His expression darkened.

"Amara."

The way he said her name was alarming. It sounded less like a name and more like a sentence handed down by a judge.

"Yes?"

"Did you break into my house?"

She considered her options. Lie? Run? Pretend to faint?

"Technically," she began, "the door was open."

His eyes flashed.

"You entered my private residence."

"I was cold."

"You stole my clothes."

"I borrowed them."

"You took my food."

"I was hungry."

"You entered a restricted territory."

She pointed at him.

"Now, that one is partly your fault."

His face went completely still.

Somewhere behind him, the gray wolf sat down. Amara had the uncomfortable feeling that it was watching a comedy show.

"How," the man asked very quietly, "is that my fault?"

"You should put up better signs! There was a wooden board back there, but it said something like 'Blackthorn' and had a picture of a wolf. I assumed it was a hiking trail."

He stared at her.

"You saw a wolf on the sign."

"Yes."

"And you continued walking."

"Well, it was a very nice drawing."

For the first time in what was probably centuries, someone had managed to leave Alpha Kael Draven speechless.

He simply looked at her.

Amara offered a weak smile.

"Are we done here? Because I have a bus to catch."

"You are not leaving."

Her smile vanished.

"Excuse me?"

"You heard me."

"Sir, with all due respect, you cannot kidnap people because they accidentally borrow your laundry."

"I am not kidnapping you."

"Wonderful."

"You are coming with me."

"That's kidnapping with better marketing."

His eyes flashed silver.

Amara's heart stumbled.

Not because she was afraid. Well, she was definitely afraid.

But there was something else.

The moment their eyes met, a warm sensation spread through her chest, delicate and sudden, like the first sunlight entering a dark room.

She pressed a hand against her heart.

What was that?

Kael went rigid.

His expression changed so quickly that she almost missed it. Shock. Confusion. And, for the briefest moment, something dangerously close to fear.

Because he had felt it too.

A single heartbeat. A strange pull. A scent that did not belong in his forest, yet somehow felt more familiar than his own blood.

His wolf stirred deep inside him.

Mate.

The word thundered through his mind.

Kael stopped breathing.

No. Absolutely not.

Not this girl. Not the ridiculous human who had broken into his house, insulted his property, and stolen his socks.

His wolf pushed forward, impatient and certain.

Our mate.

Kael looked at Amara.

She was busy trying to stuff the socks back into the paper bag.

He had faced rival Alphas, survived assassination attempts, and fought wolves twice his size.

Yet nothing in his entire life had prepared him for the horrifying possibility that the Moon Goddess had chosen this woman for him.

A human.

A chaotic, apple-stealing human.

His mate.

Kael closed his eyes.

For the first time in twenty-eight years, the Alpha of Blackthorn Pack wanted to argue with the moon.

And he had a terrible feeling the moon would win.

2. The Alpha's Very Unfortunate Problem

"Move."

Amara looked up.

"Where?"

"To the car."

She glanced around.

"What car?"

Kael pointed toward the trees.

A sleek black SUV stood beside a narrow path, its headlights illuminating the mist.

Amara's mouth fell open.

"Oh."

She looked at him.

"You're rich."

Kael stared at her.

"That is your observation?"

"I mean, look at this place. Huge mansion. Fancy car. Private forest. You probably have a refrigerator that tells you when you're sad."

He said nothing.

She nodded thoughtfully.

"Definitely rich."

"Walk."

She followed him for three steps before stopping.

"Wait."

He turned slowly.

"What now?"

"Are you a vampire?"

Kael's eyebrows drew together.

"No."

"A serial killer?"

"No."

"A drug dealer?"

His expression became murderous.

"Do I look like a drug dealer?"

Amara considered him.

"No. You look like the kind of man who would sue someone for breathing too loudly."

One of the wolves made that choking sound again.

Kael's head snapped toward it.

"Ronan."

The gray wolf lowered its ears.

Amara looked between them.

"Ronan? You named your wolf?"

Kael's expression suggested that he was reconsidering every decision that had brought him to this moment.

"He is not my wolf."

"Then why does he listen to you?"

"Because I am his Alpha."

She stared at him.

Then she laughed.

Actually laughed. A bright, delighted laugh that bounced between the trees.

Kael did not appreciate it.

"What is so amusing?"

"Your Alpha?"

"Yes."

"Like in those werewolf romance stories?"

His face hardened.

Amara laughed harder.

"Oh, my goodness. You are one of those men who role-play as supernatural creatures!"

The forest went silent.

Even the insects seemed to stop chirping.

Ronan slowly turned his head toward Kael.

The wolf's expression was almost sympathetic.

Almost.

Kael approached Amara until she had to tilt her head back to look at him.

"Listen carefully, human."

She stopped laughing.

"I am not pretending to be anything."

His voice dropped into something deep and dangerous.

"And if you value your life, you will stop speaking about things you do not understand."

For a moment, she saw something in his eyes that made her smile disappear.

He was serious.

Terrifyingly serious.

And somehow, beneath the fear, she felt a strange certainty.

This man was hiding something.

Something impossible.

Something that would change her life forever.

Amara swallowed.

"Okay," she whispered. "So... you're actually a werewolf?"

Kael held her gaze.

"Yes."

She stared at him. He stared back.

Then she pointed at Ronan.

"So he can turn into a human too?"

Ronan's ears shot up.

Kael pinched the bridge of his nose.

"I am going to regret this."

Amara's face lit up.

"Oh, my goodness. Can you imagine? What if he has a human girlfriend? Does he text her with paws? Does he—"

"Get in the car."

"Right. Sorry."

She climbed into the passenger seat.

Then she leaned back out of the open door.

"One more question."

Kael's eyes closed.

"No."

"Do werewolves shed?"

He looked at her. She looked at him.

A single second passed.

Then he shut the passenger door.

From inside the car, Amara heard him mutter something that sounded suspiciously like a prayer.

She grinned.

For someone who claimed to be an Alpha, he was surprisingly easy to annoy.

And for some reason, she suspected that annoying him was going to become one of her favorite hobbies.

3. Welcome to Blackthorn

The mansion was even more magnificent up close.

Tall black pillars framed the entrance. Warm golden light spilled from enormous windows. A fountain glittered beneath the moon, and roses climbed the stone walls in deep red clusters.

Amara pressed her face against the car window.

"Oh, wow."

Kael stepped out.

She remained seated.

He opened her door.

She stared up at him.

"Do you have a swimming pool?"

"Yes."

"A library?"

"Yes."

"A kitchen?"

He looked at her.

"Naturally."

She unbuckled her seat belt.

"Then I forgive you for the kidnapping."

"I did not kidnap you."

"Of course not, Your Highness."

His jaw tightened.

Amara smiled sweetly.

She was going to enjoy this.

Inside, the mansion smelled of cedarwood, expensive cologne, and fresh bread. A chandelier hung above the entrance, scattering golden light across polished marble floors. Portraits of stern-looking men and women lined the walls, their silver eyes seeming to follow every movement.

Amara looked at one particularly intimidating portrait.

"That one looks like he would disapprove of my entire existence."

"That was my grandfather."

"Oh."

She studied the portrait again.

"He still looks like he would disapprove of my existence."

Kael took off his coat.

"That was his favorite hobby."

Amara blinked.

Then she laughed.

It was such an unexpected answer that she almost dropped her bag.

And for a moment, just a moment, Kael watched her laugh.

Really watched her.

There was nothing calculated about it. No attempt to impress him. No nervous performance designed to win his favor.

She simply found something funny and let the happiness spill out of her.

It was disarming.

He did not like being disarmed.

"Selene," he called.

A tall woman in a dark green dress hurried down the staircase. She had elegant features, silver-blonde hair, and the sort of expression that suggested she could organize an army before breakfast.

Her eyes landed on Amara. Then on the bag. Then on Kael.

Silence.

"Who," she asked carefully, "is this?"

Kael's answer came too quickly.

"A problem."

Amara raised a hand.

"Hello. I'm Amara. The problem, apparently."

The woman blinked.

Then, to Amara's surprise, she laughed.

"Oh, I like her."

Kael looked betrayed.

"Selene."

"What? She has a sense of humor. You should try acquiring one."

Amara gasped.

"Finally! Someone who understands me."

Selene approached her and took the paper bag.

She peeked inside.

Her eyebrows rose.

"Oh, dear."

Amara grimaced.

"Before you say anything, I would like to clarify that the socks were an accident."

Selene looked at Kael.

"An accident?"

"Apparently," he replied, his voice dangerously calm, "she wandered into my bedroom, stole my clothes, ate my apples, and walked into a restricted forest."

Selene looked at Amara.

Amara nodded solemnly.

"When you say it like that, it does sound bad."

Selene burst out laughing.

Kael turned away.

He needed patience. He needed discipline. He needed a locked room where he could scream into a pillow without anyone finding out.

Most importantly, he needed to figure out why the Moon Goddess had decided to punish him with a human mate.

"Selene," he said, "prepare a guest room."

"Of course."

"And find her some proper clothes."

"Of course."

"And make certain she does not wander around the property."

Amara raised her hand.

"Question."

"No," Kael said.

"But—"

"No."

"Can I have something to eat?"

Kael stopped.

He turned around very slowly.

"You ate the apples."

"Two apples do not count as dinner."

He stared at her.

She stared back.

Then her stomach growled.

Loudly.

The sound echoed through the entrance hall.

Selene pressed her lips together.

Kael's eyes flickered toward Amara's stomach.

For the first time that evening, his expression softened. Only slightly. But Amara noticed.

"You're hungry," he said.

She crossed her arms.

"No. That was the house."

Selene laughed so hard she had to grip the staircase railing.

Kael looked toward the kitchen.

"Get her something to eat."

Amara's face brightened.

"Really?"

"Before you start eating the furniture."

"You're so kind."

"I am not kind."

"Sure."

"I am not."

"Whatever you say, handsome."

The words escaped her mouth before she could stop them.

Silence fell.

Amara's eyes widened.

Kael turned toward her.

Slowly. Very slowly.

"What did you call me?"

Her soul attempted to leave her body.

"I said... demanding."

"No, you didn't."

"I have a slight speech problem."

"You called me handsome."

Amara covered her face.

"I would like to return to the forest."

For the first time that night, Kael smiled.

It was brief. Dangerous. And so unexpectedly beautiful that Amara forgot what she had been saying.

One corner of his mouth lifted, revealing a dimple she was absolutely certain should have been illegal.

Her heart did something ridiculous.

Something fluttery.

Something deeply embarrassing.

Oh, no.

Oh, absolutely not.

She had survived a terrifying wolf, a kidnapping-that-was-not-a- kidnapping, and a mansion full of supernatural creatures.

She was not about to fall for a man who looked like he could ruin her credit score with one glance.

Kael's smile disappeared as quickly as it had come.

But not before he noticed her staring.

Interesting.

Perhaps this situation would not be entirely unbearable.

4. The Rules of Sleeping Under an Alpha's Roof

An hour later, Amara stood in a bedroom larger than the apartment she shared with her aunt.

The bed was enormous, dressed in white linen and charcoal-gray blankets. A fireplace crackled softly against one wall. Beyond the glass doors lay a balcony overlooking the forest, where moonlight spilled across the trees.

A tray of food sat on the bedside table.

Roasted chicken. Potatoes. Fresh bread. A bowl of strawberries.

And a slice of chocolate cake.

Amara stared at it with genuine emotion.

"Whoever made this food deserves a national award."

Selene stood near the door, smiling.

"The kitchen staff will be pleased to hear that."

Amara took a bite of chicken.

Her eyes closed.

"Oh, my goodness."

Another bite.

"Oh, my actual goodness."

Selene laughed.

"You're adorable."

Amara swallowed.

"Thank you. I also accept compliments in the form of extra cake."

"I'll remember that."

Selene paused. Then her expression became more serious.

"Amara, there are a few things you need to understand about this house."

Amara straightened.

"Okay."

"Do not enter the east wing after midnight."

"Why?"

"Because it is restricted."

"That is not an explanation."

"It is the one you're getting."

Amara frowned.

"Fine."

"Do not wander into the forest alone."

"Fair."

"Do not approach the Alpha when his eyes turn completely silver."

Amara stopped chewing.

"Why?"

Selene hesitated.

"Because he may not be entirely in control of his wolf."

Amara slowly put down her fork.

"Oh."

"And one more thing."

"What?"

Selene looked directly into her eyes.

"If Kael tells you to run, you run. You do not argue. You do not ask questions. You do not make jokes."

Amara swallowed.

"That sounds... concerning."

"It is."

A quiet moment passed.

Then Amara asked the question that had been troubling her since the forest.

"Is he always so angry?"

Selene's expression softened.

"No."

"Really?"

"He used to laugh more."

Something in her voice made Amara look up.

"What happened?"

Selene glanced toward the door.

"His parents died when he was young. His father was the previous Alpha. After his death, Kael was forced to become a leader before he was ready."

Amara's smile faded.

"Oh."

"He learned to protect everyone. To be strong. To never show weakness."

Selene looked back at her.

"Sometimes, when someone spends their whole life protecting their heart, they forget how to let anyone near it."

Amara thought about the man in the forest. The way he had looked at her. The brief softness in his eyes when he realized she was hungry. The tiny, unexpected smile.

"That sounds lonely," she whispered.

Selene nodded.

"It is."

Amara looked down at her plate.

For the first time since entering Blackthorn territory, she did not have a joke ready.

Then she looked up again.

"Well, I hope he likes chocolate cake."

Selene blinked.

"What?"

"Chocolate cake makes people happier. It's basically medicine."

A laugh escaped Selene.

"You are going to be trouble."

"I've been told."

Selene left a few minutes later, closing the door gently behind her.

Amara ate the rest of her dinner, washed her hands, and wandered toward the balcony.

The night was beautiful.

The forest stretched endlessly beneath the moon, and tiny lights flickered between the trees.

Fireflies.

Or something that looked like fireflies.

Amara leaned against the railing.

She should have been terrified.

She was alone in a mansion owned by a man who claimed to be a werewolf. She had discovered that monsters were real, and she had apparently offended their leader within ten minutes of meeting him.

Yet, strangely, she felt safe.

A little confused. A little frightened. But safe.

She pressed her hand against her chest again.

That strange warmth returned.

A gentle pull.

Like something inside her had recognized a melody it had been waiting to hear.

"What is wrong with me?"

Behind her, the bedroom door opened.

She turned.

Kael stood in the doorway, wearing a fresh black shirt and dark trousers. His hair was damp, as though he had just showered, and his expression was once again unreadable.

Amara's brain immediately forgot how to perform basic functions.

Look at the wall.

She looked at the wall.

Look at the ceiling.

She looked at the ceiling.

Stop looking at his shoulders.

She looked at his shoulders.

Disaster.

"Are you finished staring?" he asked.

Her head snapped up.

"I wasn't staring."

"You were."

"I was observing."

"Observing what?"

"Architecture."

He glanced around the room.

"Architecture?"

"Your shoulders are... very architectural."

The silence that followed was devastating.

Amara covered her mouth.

Please let the floor open. Please let the floor open.

Kael's eyebrows rose.

Then he laughed.

Not the brief smile from earlier. A real laugh. Deep, warm, and completely unexpected.

Amara stared at him.

For a moment, he looked younger. Less burdened. Almost carefree.

And that was far more dangerous than his anger.

Because she suddenly wanted to hear him laugh again.

Kael noticed her expression.

His laughter faded.

"What?" he asked.

She shook her head.

"Nothing."

"Then stop looking at me like that."

"Like what?"

"Like you're trying to figure me out."

Amara's smile became gentler.

"Maybe I am."

Something shifted in his expression.

For a few seconds, neither spoke.

Then Kael looked away.

"Get some sleep. You'll be taken back to the nearest road tomorrow."

Her smile faltered.

"Oh."

She had no idea why that disappointed her.

She barely knew him. He was arrogant, bossy, and unnecessarily attractive.

She should have been delighted to leave.

Instead, she found herself staring at the forest.

"Right," she said quietly. "Tomorrow."

Kael heard the change in her voice.

His wolf heard it too.

She doesn't want to leave.

Kael clenched his jaw.

That was impossible.

She was human. She belonged in the human world. Whatever strange bond had awakened between them could not be allowed to control his decisions.

He turned toward the door.

"Good night, Amara."

She looked back at him.

"Good night, Alpha."

He paused.

"Kael."

She blinked.

"What?"

"My name is Kael."

A small smile touched her lips.

"Good night, Kael."

He left.

But as the door closed, the sound of her voice followed him into the corridor.

And for the first time in years, the mansion did not feel quite so empty.

5. A Heartbeat That Should Not Exist

At precisely midnight, Kael woke with a gasp.

Pain tore through his chest.

He sat upright in bed, one hand gripping the sheets while his other pressed against his heart.

His wolf was awake. Restless. Almost frantic.

Mate.

Kael shut his eyes.

"No."

The word came out as a growl.

He had known something was wrong from the moment he met her. He had felt the bond snap into place, invisible but undeniable, like a chain forged from moonlight.

He had heard the word in his wolf's mind. He had felt the pull in his bones.

But there was something else.

Something he had never experienced before.

A second heartbeat.

Not his own.

He could feel it as clearly as he could feel his own pulse, a delicate rhythm somewhere beyond his bedroom walls.

Amara.

He swung his legs over the side of the bed.

His wolf paced inside him.

She is ours.

"She is human."

Ours.

"She knows nothing about our world."

Protect her.

Kael stood.

His eyes glowed silver in the darkness.

He had spent years avoiding the one thing every Alpha was expected to find: a mate.

He had watched his father love his mother with a devotion that made him vulnerable to enemies. He had watched that love become a weapon in the hands of people who wanted to destroy their family.

And he had sworn that no one would ever possess the power to hurt him that way.

Now the Moon Goddess had sent him a human girl with a terrible sense of direction, an impressive appetite, and absolutely no respect for private property.

A girl who had looked into his eyes without bowing.

A girl who had laughed at him.

A girl whose smile had made his chest feel warmer than it had in years.

Kael dragged a hand through his hair.

"This cannot happen."

A knock sounded at his door.

He turned sharply.

"Enter."

Ronan stepped inside, now in human form, wearing a gray T-shirt and black trousers. His dark hair was disheveled, and his expression was unusually serious.

"You felt it too," Ronan said.

Kael said nothing.

Ronan exhaled.

"The bond."

"Yes."

"She's human."

"I am aware."

"An unmarked human."

Kael's eyes flashed.

"I said I am aware."

Ronan raised both hands.

"All right. Just making sure your anger has not destroyed your hearing."

Kael glared at him.

Ronan cleared his throat.

"There's something else."

"What?"

"When you brought her into the house, the ancient crest in the east wing began glowing."

Kael went completely still.

The crest had remained dark for nearly two hundred years.

It was a relic of the first Alpha, a symbol that had been passed down through generations of the Draven bloodline.

It had not responded to any Alpha. Not even Kael's father.

"Are you certain?" he asked.

Ronan nodded.

"Silver light. The same light that appeared when she crossed the inner gates."

Kael looked toward the window.

Beyond it, the moon hung over the forest, pale and watchful.

A chill passed through him.

The bond was one thing. The crest was another.

If the ancient magic had recognized Amara, then she was not simply a human who had wandered into the wrong forest.

She was connected to something buried deep within the history of Blackthorn Pack.

Something his family had hidden.

Something that might explain why the Moon Goddess had chosen her.

"Keep this quiet," Kael ordered.

Ronan nodded.

"And the girl?"

Kael looked toward the corridor.

For a moment, he pictured Amara asleep in her enormous bed, probably dreaming about chocolate cake and insulting him in her sleep.

His mouth almost curved.

Almost.

"Nobody touches her," he said.

Ronan studied him.

"Not even you?"

Kael's expression turned lethal.

"Leave."

Ronan grinned.

"Good night, Alpha."

"Ronan."

"Leaving."

The door closed.

Kael remained standing in the darkness, listening to the distant rhythm of the heartbeat that had somehow become more important to him than his own peace.

He did not know what Amara was.

He did not know why the ancient crest had awakened.

And he certainly did not know how he was supposed to survive a lifetime with a mate who thought his shoulders were architecture.

But one thing was certain.

The Moon Goddess had chosen.

And the Alpha of Blackthorn Pack was already losing a battle he had spent his entire life preparing to win.

6. The Girl in the Forbidden Wing

At 12:17 a.m., Amara Bennett woke up hungry.

Again.

She stared at the ceiling. Then at the empty plate on the bedside table. Then at the chocolate cake crumbs.

She sighed.

"Well, that was beautiful while it lasted."

Her stomach growled.

Amara sat up.

There were two possibilities.

She could remain in bed and dream about food. Or she could find the kitchen and acquire more food.

It was not a difficult decision.

She slipped into the borrowed black T-shirt, pulled on her jeans, and quietly opened her bedroom door.

The corridor was dimly lit by golden wall lamps.

She crept forward.

One step. Two steps. Three—

A floorboard creaked.

Amara froze.

She looked left.

Nothing.

She looked right.

Nothing.

"Excellent," she whispered. "Stealth level: professional."

She continued down the corridor until she reached a staircase.

Unfortunately, she had forgotten which staircase led to the kitchen.

She chose the left one.

Then she took a wrong turn.

Then another.

And eventually found herself standing before a pair of enormous wooden doors decorated with silver carvings.

A strange warmth spread through her chest.

The same warmth she had felt when she first looked into Kael's eyes.

Amara frowned.

"What is this place?"

The silver carvings shimmered.

She took a step closer.

A whisper drifted through the air.

Not a voice. Not exactly. More like a memory brushing against the edges of her mind.

At last.

Amara's breath caught.

She touched the door.

A burst of silver light flashed beneath her fingers.

The doors opened.

Inside was a circular chamber lined with ancient books and stone pillars. At its center stood a pedestal bearing a silver crest shaped like a crescent moon surrounded by three wolves.

The crest glowed.

Amara stepped inside.

Her heart began to beat faster.

"Okay," she whispered. "This is definitely not the kitchen."

She should have turned around.

She should have run back to her bedroom.

She should have remembered Selene's warning about the east wing.

Instead, she walked toward the pedestal.

Because the moment she entered the room, she felt something.

A strange ache.

A familiarity she could not explain.

As if she had been here before.

As if the room had been waiting for her.

She reached toward the silver crest.

The instant her fingers touched it, the entire chamber erupted in light.

A deep, ancient voice filled the room.

"The blood of the Moon has returned."

Amara screamed.

Somewhere in the mansion, alarms began to ring.

Footsteps thundered through the corridors.

And far away, in his bedroom, Kael Draven felt the bond between himself and Amara flare with such force that he dropped to one knee.

His eyes turned completely silver.

His wolf roared.

PROTECT HER.

Kael surged to his feet.

For the first time in his life, fear was not about his own death.

It was about hers.

He raced through the mansion, moving faster than any human eye could follow.

He reached the east wing just as another wave of silver light burst from the chamber.

And there she stood.

Amara Bennett.

His human mate.

Her hand resting on an ancient relic that had not awakened for two centuries.

Her face pale with shock.

Her eyes glowing silver.

Kael stopped breathing.

Because the crest was not merely reacting to her.

It was answering her.

And beneath the silver light, carved into the ancient stone, words began to appear.

Words that had been hidden since the first Alpha ruled the forest.

Kael read them once.

Then again.

His blood turned cold.

"When the Alpha's heart chooses the human moon, the kingdom shall kneel—or the bloodline shall burn."

Amara looked at him.

"Kael?"

Her voice trembled.

"What is happening to me?"

He could not answer.

Because for the first time in two centuries, the ancient prophecy of Blackthorn Pack had come alive.

And the girl standing before him was at its center.

The girl he had already begun to love.

The girl he might have to destroy to save his kingdom.

Or the girl for whom he might willingly watch the entire world burn.
