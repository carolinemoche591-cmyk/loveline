Married to the Mafia King's Son

She agreed to marry a stranger to save her family. She never expected the stranger to become the one person who could destroy her—or save her.

Chapter One: The Marriage Deal

Isabella had never imagined her wedding would take place in a mansion surrounded by armed guards. She had pictured flowers, music, and a man who loved her enough to make her laugh when she cried.

Instead, she stood beside Dante Moretti, the only son of the most feared crime boss in the city.

Her father's business had collapsed beneath a mountain of debt. Dante's father offered a deal: the debt would disappear if Isabella married his son. Her father begged her to accept. Isabella agreed because she could not bear to watch her family lose their home.

Dante looked as unhappy about the arrangement as she felt.

"Let's make something clear," he said quietly before the ceremony. "I won't force you to love me. This marriage is a contract, nothing more."

"Wonderful," Isabella replied. "I've always dreamed of marrying a man with the emotional warmth of a refrigerator."

For the first time, Dante smiled.

After the wedding, he gave her a room of her own, a key to every part of the house except the basement, and one warning.

"If my father asks you questions about my work, tell him you know nothing."

"I know nothing."

"You say that very confidently."

"It's one of my talents."

Despite the expensive house and beautiful clothes, Isabella felt trapped. Dante disappeared for hours and returned with bruised knuckles. Men lowered their eyes when he passed. Conversations stopped whenever she entered a room.

Yet he never frightened her. When she had a nightmare, he sat outside her door until she fell asleep. When she burned dinner, he ate it without complaint.

"You don't have to pretend it's good," she told him.

"I've eaten worse."

"That's not comforting."

"I'm trying."

One evening, Isabella overheard Dante arguing with his father. The older man wanted him to take over the family operation fully. Dante refused.

"I won't spend my life becoming you," Dante said.

His father answered coldly, "Then your wife will learn what disobedience costs."

Isabella realised her marriage contract was not merely about money. She had been placed inside a war between a father and a son.

Chapter Two: The Man Behind the Name

As the weeks passed, Isabella discovered that Dante was secretly funding a legal clinic and helping people leave dangerous situations. He wanted to separate himself from his father's criminal empire, but leaving was not as simple as walking away.

One night, she found him sitting alone in the garden, his hand wrapped in a bloodstained cloth.

"You know," she said, sitting beside him, "most husbands bring their wives flowers."

"Would you prefer flowers?"

"I'd prefer a husband who doesn't bleed on the furniture."

He laughed, and she realised how rarely he allowed himself to do so.

They began sharing meals. He taught her how to drive his ridiculous sports car. She taught him that coffee was not supposed to taste like burnt wood. They argued over films, music, and his habit of treating every minor problem like a military operation.

Then Isabella found the real reason Dante had agreed to marry her.

Her father had not simply lost money. He had discovered evidence connecting Dante's father to a series of crimes and had threatened to expose him. The marriage deal was a way to keep Isabella's family close enough to control.

When she confronted Dante, he did not deny it.

"You knew?" she asked.

"I knew my father wanted leverage. I didn't know what your father had discovered until after the wedding."

"You let me marry you without telling me."

"I thought I could protect you."

"Everyone keeps saying that when they want to make my decisions for me."

She packed a bag and went to stay with a friend. Dante did not stop her. He sent one message: You deserved the truth. I am sorry I didn't give it to you.

Three days later, Isabella returned—not because she had forgiven him, but because she had decided she wanted to know the whole truth before choosing her future.

"No more secrets," she told him.

"No more secrets."

For the first time, Dante told her everything he knew. Together, they planned to deliver the evidence to an independent lawyer and the authorities.

They were halfway through organising the documents when the lights went out.

Someone had entered the mansion.

Chapter Three: A Different Kind of Loyalty

Dante moved Isabella behind him as footsteps crossed the hallway. His father's men had found out about the evidence, and they wanted it destroyed.

But Isabella was no longer the frightened young woman who had walked into the mansion on her wedding day. She had copied the files and sent them to the lawyer before the intruders arrived.

"The evidence is already out," she whispered.

Dante stared at her, then smiled despite the danger. "I should have known you'd be trouble."

"You married me. That was your first mistake."

They escaped through the garden passage and reached a safe location. In the days that followed, Dante cooperated with investigators, and his father's organisation began to collapse under the weight of its own secrets.

There were difficult hearings, angry relatives, and nights when Dante woke convinced that someone was coming for them. Isabella never pretended their problems had vanished. She simply reminded him that he no longer had to face them alone.

Months later, Dante placed a set of signed divorce papers on the table.

"You never chose this marriage," he said. "You should be free to choose what comes next."

Isabella looked at the papers. She remembered the fear, the lies, and the wedding that had felt like a prison sentence.

She also remembered the man who had listened when she was angry, who had accepted her leaving, and who had finally told her the truth even when it cost him everything.

"I don't want to stay because of a contract," she said.

"I know."

"I want to stay because I love you. But if we do this, we do it differently. No threats. No secrets. No deciding what's best for me without asking."

Dante's eyes filled with tears he tried unsuccessfully to hide.

"I can do that."

"Good. Because I'm still not eating your cooking."

He laughed, and she kissed him.

A year later, they held a small ceremony in the garden where they had first learned to trust one another. There were no armed guards, no debt contracts, and no powerful men making decisions for them.

They had chosen each other freely.

Dante never became a perfect man, and Isabella never became a woman who stayed silent to keep the peace. Their love was built through difficult conversations, honest apologies, and the courage to walk away from the life that had been chosen for them.

She had married the mafia king's son.

But she fell in love with the man who decided he would not become his father.
