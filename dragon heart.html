DRAGONS

A forbidden heart. An unexpected love. An ancient secret.

Chapter One:
The Dragon Who Stole a Heart

The night the dragon fell in love, the moon was wearing a crown of silver, and destiny had other plans.

The kingdom of Eldoria slept peacefully beneath a sky glittering with stars. In a little cottage at the edge of the Whispering Woods, Elara Rose was fighting with a stubborn jar of strawberry jam.

"Open!" she ordered, twisting the lid with all her strength. The jar refused.

"Listen here, you little glass demon. I have survived three winters, two terrible landlords, and a goose that tried to murder me. You are not going to defeat me."

She finally placed the jar on the table and decided to get a knife. Little did she know that, far beyond her cottage, something ancient had just opened its eyes.

✦ ✧ ✦

The Dragon in the Mountains

His eyes were golden, bright as sunlight trapped inside a storm. Black scales covered his enormous body, and magnificent wings stretched from his shoulders.

His name was Draven. He was the last Shadow Dragon of Eldoria, feared by kings, hunted by warriors, and whispered about by mothers who wanted their children to finish their vegetables.

For nearly three hundred years, Draven had lived alone. Gold meant nothing to him. Power bored him. Love was, in his opinion, a ridiculous human weakness.

Then a strange wind carried a scent into his cave. Strawberries. Fresh bread. Wildflowers after rain. Something warm stirred inside his ancient heart.

"What was that?" he growled.

A mountain goat stared at him.

"Don't look at me like that. I could eat you."

The goat sneezed and walked away.

Draven narrowed his eyes. "You are an exceptionally disrespectful animal."

For the first time in three hundred years, the most terrifying dragon in the kingdom felt as though the universe was laughing at him.

✦ ✧ ✦

The Girl Beneath the Moon

Elara Rose was twenty-one, stubborn by nature, kind by instinct, and curious in ways that frequently endangered her health. She had chestnut-brown hair, warm brown eyes, and a smile that appeared whenever she tried not to laugh.

That night, she carried a basket into her garden to collect moonflowers. The pale petals opened beneath the moonlight, while fireflies drifted between the roses.

"One day, I'm going to travel beyond these woods," she whispered. "I'll see the ocean, visit the royal city, and maybe find someone handsome, kind, and with all his teeth."

A branch snapped behind her.

"Hello?"

Two golden eyes stared at her from the darkness. A gigantic dragon stepped into the moonlight.

Elara stared. The dragon stared back. Then she raised her flower basket and said, "If you're here to rob me, I'm afraid I only have potatoes."

The dragon blinked. "Potatoes?"

"And some questionable cheese. But I should warn you that the cheese has developed a personality."

Draven had faced armies without fear, yet this little human had managed to leave him speechless.

"Why would I rob you?" he rumbled.

Elara glanced at his enormous body. "Well, look at you. You could breathe on a bank and turn the entire building into expensive charcoal."

A strange sound escaped his throat. He was laughing. Actually laughing.

"You can laugh!" Elara exclaimed.

"I was clearing my throat."

"With your entire chest?"

Draven narrowed his eyes, but Elara smiled. And something impossible happened. His ancient heart softened.

She was beautiful, not like a queen painted on palace walls, but like a warm fire on a cold night. Like home after a long journey.

"What are you called, little human?" he asked.

"Elara Rose. And I'm not that little."

Draven glanced down at her. She barely reached his knee. He wisely decided not to comment.

"I am Draven."

"Are you going to eat me?" Elara asked.

"Absolutely not. I haven't eaten a human in over two hundred years."

Elara's smile vanished. "That is not as comforting as you think it is."

"Would you prefer that I say I have never eaten one?"

"Yes!"

"Then I have never eaten one."

"You're terrible at reassuring people," she told him.

He had never heard anyone speak to him like that. Yet instead of anger, he felt an unfamiliar warmth. He wanted to hear her laugh again.

✦ ✧ ✦

An Unexpected Invitation

A cold breeze moved through the garden, and Elara shivered. Draven immediately moved one enormous wing to shield her from the wind.

"Did you just protect me from the wind?" she asked.

"It was irritating me."

"The wind?"

"Yes."

"Of course."

Elara glanced at the moonflowers in her basket. "My mother used to grow these. She said beautiful things can bloom in the darkness."

Draven became still. He had lived in darkness for centuries, but he had never considered that anything beautiful might grow there.

"Would you like some tea?" Elara asked.

"I am a dragon," he reminded her.

"I noticed. You breathe fire. That sounds useful when the kettle breaks."

"I have claws capable of tearing through stone."

"Then you can help me open my jam tomorrow."

Draven stared at her. "You are an extraordinary creature."

"Thank you. My father says I'm a menace."

"He sounds wise."

"Don't start."

Draven agreed to the tea, though he was far too large to enter the cottage. He followed Elara carefully, making certain not to crush a single flower.

He had destroyed fortresses with less effort than it took to break a twig. Yet he moved as gently as he could, terrified of damaging anything she loved.

He told himself it was only one cup of tea. One harmless evening with a human girl who offered potatoes to dragons. Surely that could do no harm.

But as Elara opened the cottage door, warm golden light spilled across the garden. Something ancient stirred beneath Draven's scales.

Far away, beneath a ruined castle, a black crystal began to glow. Someone had felt the change. Someone who had waited centuries for Draven's heart to awaken.

Someone who would do anything to make certain it never loved again.

End of Chapter One

Somewhere between a basket of moonflowers and a cup of tea, a dragon discovered that the most dangerous thing in the world was not a sword, a spell, or a kingdom at war.

It was a girl who made him want to be gentle.

And he had absolutely no idea what to do about it.
