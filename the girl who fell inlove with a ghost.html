The Girl Who Fell in Love With a Ghost

She rented a house to escape her past. She never expected the past to be waiting inside.

Chapter One: The Man in the Mirror

Amara had three rules for her new apartment: never invite strangers inside, never answer calls after midnight, and never fall in love with anyone who could not pay half the rent.

The third rule became complicated on her first night.

At exactly 12:03 a.m., she saw a man standing behind her in the bathroom mirror. He had dark hair, a crooked smile, and the expression of someone who had just remembered a terrible joke.

Amara spun around.

Nobody was there.

She looked back at the mirror. The man raised one hand and waved.

"Absolutely not," she whispered.

The reflection shrugged.

Amara ran to her bedroom, locked the door, and spent the night under three blankets. The next morning, she found a note on the kitchen table.

Sorry about last night. I forgot you could see me. — Noah

She moved out that afternoon.

Unfortunately, the landlord refused to return her deposit. Even more unfortunately, every affordable apartment in town was occupied. By sunset, Amara was back in the haunted apartment, armed with a broom and a list of questions.

"Are you dangerous?" she called.

A cupboard opened.

"That is not an answer!"

A voice came from the kitchen. "I'm dead, not rude."

Noah appeared in the doorway, looking almost ordinary except for the fact that light passed faintly through his shoulders.

He had died in the apartment seven years earlier. He remembered his name, his favourite song, and the taste of his grandmother's cooking. Everything else was blurred, as though his memories had been left out in the rain.

Amara agreed to share the apartment with him on three conditions: no appearing in mirrors while she brushed her teeth, no whispering in her ear, and absolutely no moving her phone while she was using it.

"You forgot a fourth condition," Noah said.

"What?"

"Don't fall in love with me."

Amara laughed. "Trust me. You aren't my type."

Noah smiled. "Good. I died before I learned how to do laundry."

Chapter Two: Almost Alive

Living with a ghost was inconvenient, but Noah was surprisingly good company. He could make the lights flicker whenever Amara's favourite song played, and he knew the location of every missing sock in the apartment.

He also made terrible jokes.

"Why don't ghosts lie?" he asked one evening.

"Because they have nothing to hide?"

"Because you can see right through them."

Amara threw a cushion at him. It passed through his chest and struck the wall.

"Violence," Noah said solemnly. "Against a man who can't even file a complaint."

She began talking to him about things she had never told anyone: the boyfriend who had cheated on her, the job she had lost, and the fear that everyone eventually grew tired of her. Noah listened without interrupting.

In return, he told her about the life he could barely remember. He had loved the sea, hated bitter coffee, and once planned to travel across the world. He had never made it farther than the town where he was born.

One rainy evening, Amara found an old photograph behind a loose floorboard. It showed Noah standing beside a young woman with the same eyes as Amara.

"Who is she?"

Noah stared at the picture. For the first time since they met, his smile disappeared.

"My sister. Her name was Ruth."

He remembered an argument, a storm, and a door slamming. Then nothing.

Amara searched old newspaper archives and discovered that Noah had died in a fire. The official report blamed faulty wiring. But an article mentioned that his sister had accused someone of locking the building's back door.

Amara found Ruth, now an elderly woman living near the coast. When she showed her the photograph, Ruth began to cry.

"My brother was trying to save me," Ruth whispered. "He went back into the building because I was trapped. I survived. He didn't."

That night, Amara told Noah everything.

He stood perfectly still, as if the truth had become a weight he could finally put down.

"I remembered being afraid," he said. "I thought I had died alone."

Amara reached for his hand. Her fingers passed through his at first, but Noah concentrated. For one brief second, his hand became warm enough for her to hold.

Neither of them spoke.

Then Amara whispered, "I think I'm falling in love with you."

Noah looked at her with an expression that made the whole apartment feel quiet.

"I think I already did."

Chapter Three: One Last Morning

After Ruth visited the apartment and said goodbye to her brother's photograph, Noah began to change. The flickering lights stopped. His reflection appeared more clearly in mirrors. Sometimes, when Amara woke, he stood by the window watching the sunrise.

"Are you getting stronger?" she asked.

"I think I'm remembering how to leave."

The words hurt more than she expected.

They spent their final night together talking about the future she would have and the one he had never received. Noah asked her to travel, to laugh loudly, to stop apologising for taking up space. Amara asked him to stay.

"I can't promise that," he said. "But I can promise you something else. Meeting you was the best thing that happened to me after I died."

She laughed through her tears. "That's a terrible romantic line."

"I'm dead. My options are limited."

At dawn, Noah became almost transparent. Amara held his hand while sunlight filled the room.

"Will I ever see you again?" she asked.

"I don't know. But don't spend your life waiting for a ghost."

"You are incredibly annoying for someone I'm crying over."

"You have excellent taste in annoying people."

He kissed her forehead. She felt a warm breeze against her skin, the faintest suggestion of an embrace.

Then Noah was gone.

Amara stayed in the apartment for another month. She eventually moved to a seaside town and began writing the travel stories she had always dreamed of creating. She fell in love again years later, this time with a living man who knew how to wash dishes.

But she never forgot Noah.

On the anniversary of his final morning, she visited the old apartment. The new tenant had never seen a ghost. The mirror was ordinary, the lights stayed on, and the kitchen was silent.

Amara smiled at her reflection.

Then, just for a second, a familiar crooked smile appeared behind her shoulder.

She turned.

Nobody was there.

On the windowsill lay a small note, written in handwriting she would recognise anywhere.

Keep travelling, Amara. And please stop dating men who can't cook.

She laughed until she cried.

Some love stories lasted forever. Others lasted only long enough to teach someone how to live.
