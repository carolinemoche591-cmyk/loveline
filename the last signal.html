The Last Signal

Some things should never have fallen from the sky. Some secrets should never have been found.

Written by Loveline

Chapter One

The Sound Above

2:17 a.m.
✦

At exactly 2:17 in the morning, Alex Blackwood woke to the sound of something screaming across the sky.

He opened his eyes but didn't move.

For several seconds he stared at the ceiling of his small flat in London, listening to the strange metallic sound rolling through the night.

It wasn't thunder.

It wasn't an aircraft.

And it certainly wasn't something he had heard before.

"Brilliant," Alex whispered. "Exactly what I needed at two in the morning."

He sat up.

Rain tapped softly against the window. Beyond the glass, London was almost completely still.

Almost.

A red glow appeared between the clouds.

Alex stood.

The glow became brighter.

Then something moved through the clouds.

It was falling.

But it wasn't falling like a meteor.

It was too slow.

Too deliberate.

Almost as if something was controlling its descent.

Alex stepped closer to the window.

"What the hell are you?"

The object disappeared beyond the rooftops.

Three seconds later, the world shook.

The sky had just delivered something that wasn't supposed to exist.

Alex grabbed his coat.

He didn't know why.

Perhaps curiosity.

Perhaps stupidity.

In hindsight, he would later decide it had been both.

He opened his apartment door and walked into the cold hallway.

The lights flickered.

Once.

Twice.

Then every light went out.

"Oh, that's encouraging."

Somewhere below him, glass shattered.

Alex froze.

A woman's voice screamed from the street.

Then came another sound.

A low metallic hum.

Alex looked through the stairwell window.

Blue light illuminated the clouds.

Something had landed.

And whatever it was...

people were already coming for it.

Chapter Two

The Thing in the Rain

It wasn't a meteor.
✦

Alex found it in an abandoned construction site three streets from his flat.

The rain had become heavier.

He stood behind a concrete wall, breathing quietly as water ran down the back of his neck.

In front of him was a crater.

At the centre of the crater sat the object.

It wasn't round.

It wasn't shaped like any machine Alex had ever seen.

It looked as if someone had taken several pieces of black metal and folded them into a shape that mathematics itself shouldn't have allowed.

Thin blue cracks travelled across its surface.

The light inside those cracks moved.

Not randomly.

It moved like something was alive inside it.

Unknown Object

No markings. No manufacturer. No visible power source. No explanation for why it was still warm after falling from the sky.

Alex slowly climbed into the crater.

His instincts told him to leave.

Unfortunately, Alex had never been particularly good at listening to his instincts.

"Right. Quick look, then I'm going home."

He reached toward it.

The object moved.

Alex immediately pulled his hand away.

"Nope."

It moved again.

"I said nope."

The object suddenly became silent.

Alex frowned.

Then a voice spoke inside his head.

"You came back."

Alex stopped breathing.

"I have never been here before."

The voice answered.

"You have."

Alex stared at the object.

"When?"

The blue light inside the object became brighter.

A memory appeared in Alex's mind.

He was a child.

He was standing in a forest.

Someone was holding his hand.

Someone whose face he couldn't remember.

Above them...

the same object was hanging in the sky.

Alex stumbled backward.

"That's impossible."

The voice whispered:

The First Warning

"You were never supposed to remember."

Chapter Three

The Men in Grey

Someone had been waiting for him.
✦

Alex heard the vehicles before he saw them.

Four black SUVs stopped outside the construction site.

Doors opened simultaneously.

Men stepped out wearing dark grey tactical uniforms.

No police markings.

No military insignia.

Just a silver symbol on their shoulders.

A circle surrounding a vertical line.

Alex's stomach tightened.

He had seen that symbol before.

In the memory.

"Oh, that's not good."

One of the men raised a weapon.

Alex ducked.

The shot struck the concrete wall.

"Definitely not good."

He ran.

Another shot.

Then another.

Alex jumped over a fallen barrier and slid through the mud.

He expected to hear footsteps behind him.

Instead...

everything became silent.

Alex stopped.

The rain had stopped.

The men had stopped.

Even the city seemed to have stopped.

A single raindrop hung in the air beside Alex's face.

He reached toward it.

His finger touched the frozen drop.

Time suddenly returned.

The raindrop fell.

The men fired.

Alex raised his hand without thinking.

A wave of blue energy exploded outward.

Every weapon flew from the soldiers' hands.

Alex stared at his palm.

Blue light crawled beneath his skin.

"What did I just do?"

One of the soldiers whispered:

"The Blackwood subject is awake."

Alex heard the sentence.

And for the first time that night, he became genuinely afraid.

"Subject?"

He looked at his glowing hand.

"What the hell have I been?"

Chapter Four

The Woman Who Knew

She had been looking for him for years.
✦

Alex reached the river just before dawn.

He was exhausted.

His coat was torn.

His hands were still glowing faintly.

And he had absolutely no idea what was happening.

Then he saw her.

A woman stood beneath an old stone bridge.

She wore a long dark coat.

Her black hair moved slightly in the wind.

She looked at Alex as if she had been expecting him.

"Alex Blackwood."

Alex stopped.

"Who are you?"

She didn't answer immediately.

Instead, she looked at his hands.

"It's inside you."

Alex looked at her.

"What's inside me?"

She stepped closer.

"The thing that fell from the sky."

Alex's face hardened.

"How do you know about it?"

She looked toward the clouds.

Far above them, something enormous moved behind the storm.

Her voice became quieter.

The Woman's Warning

"Because I've spent twelve years trying to stop it from finding you."

Alex stared at her.

"Twelve years?"

She nodded.

"And now I've failed."

Alex looked toward the sky.

The clouds were moving strangely.

Something was coming.

Something enormous.

Alex turned back to her.

"Who are you?"

The woman finally answered.

"Evelyn."

She paused.

"And if you want to survive the next forty-eight hours..."

She looked directly into his eyes.

"...you need to trust me."

Alex gave her a tired look.

"That's a terrible opening line."

For the first time, Evelyn smiled.

It lasted less than a second.

Then the sky thundered.

And both of them looked up.

Chapter Five

Something Is Coming

The hunters had arrived.
✦

The first explosion came at sunrise.

Windows shattered across the city.

Birds disappeared from the sky.

Cars stopped in the middle of the roads.

Every electronic device in London displayed the same strange symbol.

A circle.

A line.

And beneath it...

one message.

Emergency Transmission

RUN FROM THE LIGHT.

Alex stared at the message.

Evelyn grabbed his arm.

"Don't look at the sky."

Alex slowly looked at her.

"Why?"

Evelyn's expression changed.

"Because it can see you."

Alex looked upward anyway.

He shouldn't have.

But curiosity had already ruined his life once.

He wasn't about to stop now.

Above the clouds was something impossible.

Not a plane.

Not a satellite.

Not a conventional spacecraft.

It was enormous.

Silent.

Dark enough to make the clouds around it appear pale.

And beneath it...

thousands of tiny lights moved like stars.

Alex felt the object inside him react.

His chest became painfully hot.

The voice returned.

"They have found us."

Alex whispered:

"Who?"

The voice answered:

The Last Warning

"Everyone."

Chapter Six

The Blackwood File

Alex was not the first.
✦

Beneath an abandoned government facility, someone opened a file that had been sealed for twenty-one years.

The folder contained only three photographs.

The first showed a forest.

The second showed the strange object.

The third showed a young boy.

The boy was Alex.

He was eight years old.

Standing beside him was a woman whose face had been scratched out.

Beneath the photograph were six words.

SUBJECT SURVIVED THE FIRST CONTACT.

The government scientist holding the file whispered:

"That's impossible."

A voice behind him answered:

"No."

The scientist turned.

A tall figure stood in the doorway.

Its face was hidden beneath a dark mask.

"What's impossible..."

The figure stepped forward.

"...is that he survived twice."

The lights went out.

Something screamed in the corridor.

Then the file disappeared.

The Story Continues

More Secrets Await

Nothing about Alex Blackwood is what it seems.
✦

Alex thought the object had changed his life.

He was wrong.

The object hadn't changed his life.

It had revealed it.

Somewhere beneath the cities, governments were opening sealed laboratories.

Somewhere in the darkness, creatures that had watched humanity for centuries were waking.

And somewhere beyond the stars, something was travelling toward Earth.

Something that knew Alex's name.

The Question

What really fell from the sky?

Alex would eventually discover the answer.

But by then...

the world would already be changing.
