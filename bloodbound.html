Bloodbound

He was born with the hunger of a vampire, the fire of a dragon, and a destiny written in the blood of ancient enemies. She was raised to kill him. Loving him might destroy them both.

Written by Loveline

Prologue: The Night the Moon Bled

The moon turned red on the night Prince Kael was born.

Across the five kingdoms, creatures that had slept for centuries opened their eyes. Wolves stopped howling in the northern forests. The fae abandoned their dances beneath the silver trees. Deep beneath the mountains, dragons stirred, their enormous bodies shifting against the stone that had hidden them from the world.

In Veyr, capital of the vampire kingdom, every candle in the royal palace died at the same moment.

Queen Seraphine lay exhausted beneath black silk sheets, holding a newborn child against her chest. The baby did not cry. He stared at the world with one silver eye and one crimson eye, while a thin ribbon of blue fire danced across his tiny fingers.

The royal physician fell to his knees.

"Your Majesty," he whispered. "This child should not exist."

King Valerius stood beside the bed, dressed in black velvet and a crown made from the bones of ancient kings. He had ruled the vampires for three hundred years. He had conquered cities, broken armies, and forced powerful families to bow before him.

Yet he stared at his son as if the child were the one thing in the world he could not control.

"Explain," the king ordered.

The physician swallowed. "The silver eye belongs to the dragon bloodline. The crimson eye belongs to your own. His magic is awakening before he has even taken his first breath."

"Then suppress it."

"It cannot be suppressed. The old prophecy—"

"I did not ask about a prophecy."

The physician lowered his head.

Seraphine pulled the child closer. Before she became queen, she had loved a dragon prince named Auren. Their love had been forbidden, their meetings hidden, and their final separation bloody. The kingdoms believed Auren had died in the war between vampires and dragons.

But his blood lived in the child she held.

"His name is Kael," she said.

Valerius looked at her. "He will be raised as my son. No one will speak of what he is."

"And when he asks?"

"You will tell him what keeps him obedient."

Seraphine's eyes hardened. "He is a child, not a weapon."

"Every heir is a weapon. The only question is who holds the handle."

Outside, thunder shook the palace. In a tower abandoned since the first vampire war, a sealed book opened by itself. Its pages were blank until silver letters appeared, forming a prophecy in a language older than the kingdoms.

When vampire blood and dragon fire share one heart, the five kingdoms shall kneel or rise. The heir shall find his equal in the hand that carries the blade. If they choose hatred, the world will burn. If they choose love, the old order will fall.

Far away, in a village hidden beneath the northern mountains, a baby girl woke and began to cry.

A crescent-shaped mark appeared on her palm.

The old woman who held her stared at the mark and whispered a name she had hoped never to speak again.

"The marked one."

Chapter One: The Prince Who Could Not Feed

On the morning of his twentieth birthday, Prince Kael Veyr discovered a dead raven on his pillow, a crack running through his bedroom mirror, and a small blue flame eating its way across the expensive curtains.

He stared at the fire.

"Not today."

The flame grew.

"I mean it."

The flame climbed the curtain rod.

Kael sighed, reached out, and closed his fist. The fire vanished instantly, leaving a smoking hole in the fabric.

Someone knocked on the door.

"Your Highness?" called Tavin, his closest friend and the only person in the palace who seemed determined to survive by irritating him. "Are you alive?"

"Unfortunately."

"Wonderful. May I enter, or are you currently destroying another part of the palace?"

"Enter."

Tavin stepped inside carrying a breakfast tray. He was a tall, thin young man with untidy hair, a clever smile, and an alarming belief that jokes could solve almost any problem.

He noticed the raven.

Then the curtains.

Then the mirror.

"I have questions," he announced.

"You always have questions."

"Yes, but today they are particularly important. Why is your pillow occupied by a dead bird? Why are your curtains smoking? And should I be worried that your reflection appears to have grown horns?"

Kael turned toward the mirror.

For one terrible second, his reflection did not look like him. Enormous black wings spread behind his shoulders. Silver scales shimmered along his throat, and blue fire burned inside his eyes.

Then the reflection returned to normal.

Kael gripped the edge of the table.

"You saw nothing."

Tavin set down the tray. "I saw several things, all of which I intend to forget for the sake of our friendship."

"Good."

"However, I would like to mention that if you become a giant monster, I expect a generous salary increase."

Despite himself, Kael laughed.

It was the sort of laughter he rarely allowed in public. At court, every smile was studied, every word repeated, and every weakness collected by someone who hoped to use it later.

Kael had learned early that a prince could be surrounded by hundreds of people and still be completely alone.

He picked up the goblet on the tray. It contained bloodwine, the traditional drink of the vampire royal family. Its dark scent filled the room.

He took a sip and immediately grimaced.

"Still terrible?" Tavin asked.

"It tastes like a rusty sword soaked in vinegar."

"Perhaps the kitchen has changed the recipe."

"The kitchen has followed the same recipe for eight hundred years."

"Then perhaps your taste buds are ungrateful."

Kael pushed the goblet away. He had never been able to drink bloodwine without pain. His father called it a weakness. The royal physicians called it a rare disorder. Kael suspected the truth was connected to the strange fire that sometimes woke inside him.

His hunger was unlike that of other vampires. Blood did not satisfy it. It only awakened something enormous and furious beneath his skin.

For years, he had hidden the problem.

He was not sure how much longer he could.

The palace bells rang.

Once.

Twice.

Then a third time, although no ceremony had been scheduled.

Kael felt the sound inside his bones.

The raven on his pillow burst into blue flame. Its feathers vanished, leaving a small silver ring behind. The ring rolled toward him and stopped against his hand.

When he touched it, a woman's voice whispered inside his mind.

"Find the girl with the moon scar."

Kael snatched his hand away.

"What happened?" Tavin asked.

"Nothing."

"You look like you have just heard the dead speak."

Kael stared at the ring.

"That is because I think I have."

Outside the palace walls, a stranger had entered the city carrying a silver dagger beneath her cloak.

Her name was Elara Moon.

And she had been trained since childhood to put that dagger through Kael's heart.

Chapter Two: The Girl with the Silver Blade

Elara had never trusted beautiful things.

Beautiful things distracted people. They made them careless. A beautiful face could hide a cruel heart; a beautiful palace could conceal a prison; a beautiful promise could lead a person straight into a grave.

She had learned that lesson before she was old enough to understand the word betrayal.

Her parents died during a raid on her village. She remembered fire in the windows, the sound of horses, and her mother's hands pushing her into a cellar beneath the floorboards.

"Whatever happens," her mother had whispered, "do not let them turn your heart into a weapon."

Elara had never forgotten those words.

But when the Moon Order found her, they taught her something different.

They told her that vampires had destroyed her family. They told her that mercy was a luxury the dead could not afford. They trained her to fight, to track, to lie, and to kill without allowing fear to interfere.

By the age of nineteen, she could cross a guarded courtyard without being seen. She could hit a moving target from across a room. She could recognize the scent of a vampire from several streets away.

She had never killed a prince.

Master Orren, leader of the Moon Order, summoned her before dawn and placed a narrow silver dagger on the table.

"The vampire heir has awakened," he said.

Elara examined the blade. Symbols had been carved along its handle.

"What makes him different from the others?"

"He carries dragon blood. If he masters it, he could command fire powerful enough to destroy every kingdom."

"Then why hasn't he already done so?"

"Because his power is still sleeping."

Orren's expression was severe. He had raised Elara after her parents died. She had trusted him more than anyone alive.

"You will enter the palace as a servant," he continued. "You will learn his routines, discover his weaknesses, and wait for the right moment."

"And if I refuse?"

Orren looked surprised.

Elara had never asked that question before.

"Then the Order will find someone else," he replied. "But understand this: if the prince survives, the world may not."

He handed her a folded piece of parchment.

It contained a drawing of a young man with dark hair and mismatched eyes.

Kael Veyr.

Elara studied the drawing. She expected to feel hatred. Instead, a strange sadness settled over her, as though she were remembering a person she had never met.

That night, she dreamed of standing in a burning hall. A young man reached for her through the smoke.

"You came back," he said.

She woke with tears on her face and her dagger clutched in her hand.

By sunrise, she had convinced herself it meant nothing.

She entered Veyr wearing a servant's plain grey dress, her hair tucked beneath a scarf. The city was crowded with merchants, winged messengers, and creatures whose shadows moved in directions their bodies did not.

At the palace gates, a guard looked her up and down.

"Name?"

"Elara."

"Family?"

"Dead."

The guard hesitated.

"You could have said you had none."

"I could have."

He stared at her, apparently uncertain whether she was grieving or rude.

At last, he waved her through.

Elara crossed the courtyard and entered the palace. She could feel the dagger against her ankle with every step.

Somewhere behind those walls was the prince she had been ordered to kill.

She told herself that she was ready.

She did not yet know that the most dangerous part of her mission would be the moment Kael looked at her without fear.

Chapter Three: The Library That Bit Back

Elara found the prince in the royal library, arguing with a book.

"You cannot be serious," Kael told the open volume. "Seven hundred pages on blood magic, and not one useful sentence about how to stop a headache."

The book snapped shut on his fingers.

"Ow! Treacherous paper."

Elara paused in the doorway.

The prince looked nothing like the monster described in the Order's warnings. He looked tired, irritated, and deeply offended by stationery.

He noticed her.

"You are new."

"I started this morning."

"That explains it. Everyone else knows not to enter this room when I am losing an argument."

"Perhaps the book has a point."

Kael stared at her.

Then he laughed.

"What is your name?"

"Elara."

The moment she spoke, the mark on her palm burned beneath her glove. Kael's hand moved to his wrist, where an identical mark had appeared that morning.

Neither spoke of it.

Instead, Kael gestured toward a stack of books.

"Do you know anything about dragon magic?"

"Only that dragons dislike being hunted."

"That is an excellent start. I have been trying to learn why my hands occasionally catch fire."

"Perhaps you should stop touching things."

"I have considered it. Unfortunately, I enjoy touching things."

Elara almost smiled.

She was still deciding whether to hate him when the library doors exploded inward.

Three creatures rushed through the opening. They were made of smoke, bone, and darkness, with mouths full of sharp white teeth.

Kael shoved Elara behind him.

"Stay back!"

The first creature lunged. Kael thrust out his hand, releasing a burst of blue fire. The creature split apart, then reformed behind a bookshelf.

Elara drew her silver dagger.

The second creature charged her. She ducked beneath its claws and cut through its shadow. It dissolved with a shriek.

Kael glanced at her weapon.

"That is not a kitchen knife."

"You noticed."

The final creature wrapped itself around Kael's arm.

He screamed.

Silver scales spread across his throat. His shoulders widened, and enormous black wings burst through his shirt. The windows shattered. Every book in the library flew into the air.

For one terrifying moment, Elara saw the creature the Order had warned her about.

Then she saw the fear in his eyes.

Not fear of dying.

Fear of hurting someone.

She ran toward him and drove her dagger into the shadow creature's chest.

Blue fire erupted across the room. The creature vanished.

Silence followed.

Kael stood among the fallen books, his wings slowly folding behind him. His eyes were entirely crimson.

Elara raised her dagger.

This was the moment. One strike. One death. One completed mission.

Kael looked at the blade.

"You were sent to kill me."

She tightened her grip.

"Yes."

"Are you going to?"

Elara thought of her parents. She thought of Orren. She thought of the prince placing himself between her and a creature that could have killed them both.

"Not today."

Kael lowered his hands.

"Then I suppose we should introduce ourselves properly. I am Kael. Unwilling prince, terrible student, and owner of a library that apparently hates visitors."

Elara lowered her dagger.

"Elara. Unwilling visitor, terrible liar, and apparently the person who just saved your life."

He smiled.

"I think we will get along."

Elara turned away before he could see the fear in her face.

She had spared her target.

And somewhere beneath her training, beneath her anger, something had begun to change.
