Chapter One: The Girl Who Smelled Like Sunshine

The rain began at midnight.

It fell over the little town of Blackthorn like the sky was trying to wash away a thousand forgotten sins. Water slipped down the rooftops, whispered against the windows, and gathered in the narrow streets where the old streetlights flickered like dying stars.

Everyone in Blackthorn knew the rules.

Never walk through the forest after dark. Never answer a voice calling your name from the shadows. And whatever you do, never fall in love with someone whose heart stopped beating two hundred years ago.

Unfortunately for Adrian Vale, the last rule was about to become a problem.

A very beautiful problem.

A problem with warm brown eyes, a stubborn mouth, and absolutely no sense of self-preservation.

Her name was Elena Rose.

And she was late for work.

"Of all the nights for my umbrella to betray me," Elena muttered, holding a tiny, broken umbrella over her head as the rain soaked her hair. "I hope whoever invented rain is having a terrible evening."

She hurried down the deserted street, clutching her handbag against her chest. Her shoes splashed through puddles, and a strand of dark curls stuck to her cheek.

She stopped beneath a streetlight and checked her phone.

11:47 p.m.

"Oh, wonderful. Eleven minutes before my manager turns into a dragon."

She sighed.

Then the streetlight above her went out.

Elena froze.

A strange silence settled over the road. Even the rain seemed to soften, as if the night itself were holding its breath.

She glanced over her shoulder.

Nothing.

"Hello?" she called.

A foolish question. She knew it was foolish the moment it left her mouth.

From somewhere behind her came a voice.

"Do you always speak to empty streets?"

Elena spun around.

A man stood beneath the shadow of an old stone building.

He was tall, dressed in a black coat that fell almost to his knees. His dark hair was damp from the rain, though he seemed not to notice it. His face was striking in a way that felt almost unfair: sharp cheekbones, a perfectly defined jaw, and lips that looked as though they had forgotten how to smile.

But it was his eyes that caught her.

Pale silver.

Not gray. Not blue.

Silver, like moonlight trapped beneath ice.

Elena stared at him for a second longer than she intended.

Then she lifted her broken umbrella.

"Only when handsome strangers appear out of nowhere. It helps me determine whether I'm dreaming or about to be murdered."

One of his eyebrows rose.

"Which do you suspect?"

"Honestly? You're dressed like both."

For one brief moment, the stranger looked genuinely surprised.

Then he laughed.

It was a quiet, unfamiliar sound, as though laughter were something he had not used in a very long time.

Elena smiled before she could stop herself.

And Adrian Vale, who had spent two hundred and seventeen years avoiding humanity, felt something inside his chest move.

Not his heart.

That had been silent for centuries.

Something else.

Something far more dangerous.

"Your name?" he asked.

"Elena."

"Just Elena?"

"Would you like my birth certificate too?"

His lips curved.

"Perhaps later."

"Oh, you're one of those."

"One of what?"

"Men who think being attractive is a substitute for having a personality."

Adrian blinked.

Then, to his own astonishment, he laughed again.

If Elena had known that she was the first person in decades to make the immortal Lord of Blackthorn laugh, she might have felt considerably more powerful.

Instead, she was busy trying to rescue her umbrella from turning inside out.

✦ ✦ ✦

The first thing Adrian noticed about Elena was her scent.

Not perfume. Not soap.

Something warmer.

Something that reminded him of summer mornings, freshly baked bread, and sunlight spilling across an open window.

Human blood had always called to him. He had spent centuries learning to ignore its sweet, terrible invitation.

But Elena's scent was different.

It did not awaken his hunger.

It awakened his memory.

For one impossible second, he remembered what it had felt like to be alive.

To have a heartbeat.

To breathe because his body needed air, rather than because he wanted to imitate the living.

To stand in the sun without fearing what it would do to him.

He had forgotten all those things.

Until her.

Then Elena shivered.

Adrian immediately removed his coat.

"Here."

She glanced at it, then at him.

"You'll get cold."

"I won't."

"That's what everyone says before they start sneezing."

"I don't sneeze."

"Of course you don't. You're probably one of those people who doesn't sweat either."

"Are you always this suspicious?"

"Only when strange men offer me clothing in dark alleys."

"Would you prefer to remain in the rain?"

Elena considered this.

"No. But if you turn out to be a serial killer, I'm haunting you."

"An interesting threat."

"I have many."

He draped the coat over her shoulders before she could object.

It was surprisingly warm.

It smelled faintly of cedarwood, rain, and something she could not identify. Something old, elegant, and strangely comforting.

Elena pulled the coat closer around herself.

For reasons she couldn't explain, she suddenly felt safe.

That should have worried her.

It did not.

✦ ✦ ✦

"Where are you going?" Adrian asked as Elena started walking.

"Moonlight Café."

He went still.

"The café near the old cemetery?"

She looked back.

"Yes. Do you know it?"

"Unfortunately."

"Why unfortunately?"

"Because it is a terrible place to be at midnight."

Elena folded her arms.

"I work there."

"My condolences."

She gasped.

"Excuse me? The coffee is excellent."

"I have never tasted it."

"Then your opinion is irrelevant."

"Perhaps."

"And for your information, the owner makes the best cinnamon rolls in the entire town."

Adrian stared at her.

"You are defending a café that serves coffee beside a cemetery."

"And cinnamon rolls. Try to keep up."

She turned and continued walking.

Adrian remained where he was.

He should have left her there.

He should have returned to his estate, locked the doors, and forgotten that the girl with the broken umbrella existed.

Instead, he followed her.

Not because he was concerned.

Certainly not because he wanted to hear her speak again.

He followed because the streets were dangerous.

Because the town had enemies.

Because something in the wind felt wrong.

That was what he told himself.

The fact that he could have crossed the entire town in less than a minute, yet chose to walk beside her at the pace of a human, was none of his concern.

"So," Elena said, glancing at him, "do you have a name, or should I call you Mr. Mysterious Coat?"

"Adrian."

"Adrian what?"

"Vale."

She repeated it quietly.

"Adrian Vale."

Something about the name felt familiar, though she couldn't remember where she had heard it.

Then she snapped her fingers.

"Wait! Vale Manor?"

He looked at her.

"The enormous house on the hill? The one everyone says is haunted?"

"It is not haunted."

"Oh, good. So you live there alone because you enjoy the peaceful company of ghosts?"

"I live alone because I prefer silence."

Elena looked at him with exaggerated sympathy.

"That must be exhausting."

"Why?"

"Because you clearly have no idea how to enjoy yourself."

Adrian gave her a sideways glance.

"And you do?"

"Absolutely. I enjoy good food, warm blankets, romantic movies, and men who can smile without looking as though they're planning someone's funeral."

"Your standards are demanding."

"My standards are excellent."

He almost smiled again.

Almost.

Then they reached the old stone bridge.

The café was visible on the other side, its windows glowing amber against the darkness.

Elena stopped.

"That's me."

Adrian looked toward the building.

Something moved between the trees behind it.

Something tall.

Something that should not have been moving.

His expression changed.

The warmth vanished from his face. His silver eyes darkened until they looked almost black.

"Adrian?"

He did not answer.

"Elena," he said quietly, "when you enter that café, lock the door behind you."

She frowned.

"Why?"

"Just do it."

"Are you going to tell me what's happening?"

"No."

"Wow. You really are terrible at communication."

"Elena."

There was something in his voice now that made her stop joking.

It was not anger.

It was fear.

And somehow, that frightened her more.

"Fine," she whispered. "I'll lock the door."

"Good."

She hesitated, then removed his coat from her shoulders.

"Here. Thank you for walking me."

He did not take it.

"Keep it."

"But—"

"Please."

The word surprised her.

Adrian Vale did not ask.

He commanded. He instructed. He made decisions and expected the world to follow them.

Yet he had said please.

Elena slipped the coat back on.

"Good night, Adrian."

He looked at her as though those three words were something he had been waiting centuries to hear.

"Good night, Elena."

She hurried toward the café.

At the door, she glanced back.

He was still standing on the bridge, watching her.

She raised one hand and waved.

He did not wave back.

But he stayed until she disappeared inside.

Only then did he turn toward the trees.

The creature was gone.

In its place lay a small black feather.

Adrian crouched and picked it up.

His fingers tightened around it.

"No," he whispered.

The feather crumbled into ash.

He knew that magic.

He knew that scent.

And he knew exactly who had sent it.

Someone had discovered Elena Rose.

Someone knew what she was.

And if Adrian was right, the girl who had just threatened to haunt him was already in terrible danger.

✦ ✦ ✦

Inside the Moonlight Café, Elena was busy fighting with the coffee machine.

"Come on," she pleaded. "Please don't do this to me. I have rent to pay, dreams to achieve, and absolutely no money for a replacement."

The machine made a horrible grinding noise.

Elena closed her eyes.

"I swear, if you break, I'm becoming a farmer."

"Talking to appliances now?"

Elena nearly jumped out of her skin.

Her best friend, Maya, stood behind the counter with a tray of clean cups.

Elena pressed a hand to her chest.

"You almost killed me!"

"Please. You scream when the microwave beeps."

"It beeps aggressively."

Maya laughed, then noticed the black coat.

She slowly lowered the tray.

"Elena."

"What?"

"Whose coat is that?"

Elena glanced down.

"Oh. Adrian's."

Maya stared at her.

"Adrian who?"

"Vale."

The tray slipped from Maya's hands.

Several cups crashed onto the floor.

Elena flinched.

"Maya!"

Her friend did not look at the broken cups.

She was staring at Elena as though she had just announced that she had adopted a crocodile.

"You met Adrian Vale?"

"Technically, he met me. I was having an argument with my umbrella."

"Elena, listen to me very carefully."

"Oh, here we go."

"Take that coat off."

"Why?"

"Because Adrian Vale is not a man you should be walking around wearing."

Elena frowned.

"Is he married?"

"What? No!"

"Then what's the problem?"

Maya grabbed her by the shoulders.

"The problem is that nobody has seen him age. My grandmother said he looked exactly the same when she was a little girl. There are stories about his family, Elena. Terrible stories."

"People say terrible things about everyone."

"Not like this."

Maya lowered her voice.

"They say he drinks blood."

Elena stared at her.

Then she laughed.

Not a little laugh.

A full, helpless laugh that made Maya look personally offended.

"Blood?" Elena repeated. "What does he drink, then? Coffee with a side of human neck?"

"I'm serious!"

"So am I. Have you seen his cheekbones? If he drinks anything, it should be water and a healthy amount of humility."

Maya groaned.

"You are impossible."

"And you are dramatic."

But when Elena turned toward the window, her smile faded.

Across the street, beneath the flickering streetlight, stood Adrian Vale.

He was watching the café.

Watching her.

For one strange moment, their eyes met through the rain-streaked glass.

Then a car passed between them.

When it was gone, Adrian had disappeared.

Elena touched the collar of his coat.

It still carried the warmth of his scent.

For the first time that night, she wondered whether Maya's stories might contain a little truth.

✦ ✦ ✦

At the top of the hill, Vale Manor stood beneath the moon like a forgotten kingdom.

Its windows were dark. Its gardens were overgrown. Ivy climbed the ancient stone walls, and the iron gates bore a family crest no living historian could explain.

Adrian entered the manor without turning on a single light.

He did not need them.

He crossed the entrance hall, passed portraits of dead relatives, and stopped before a tall mirror framed in black wood.

His reflection stared back at him.

Pale skin.

Silver eyes.

A face unchanged by two centuries of death.

He loosened his collar.

Then he placed one hand against his chest.

Nothing.

No pulse.

No warmth.

No heartbeat.

He closed his eyes.

And suddenly, he could hear her again.

Elena laughing beneath the streetlight.

Elena arguing with her umbrella.

Elena saying his name.

Adrian opened his eyes sharply.

"Impossible."

A voice answered from the darkness.

"Love usually is."

Adrian turned.

An old woman stood at the foot of the staircase. Her silver hair fell over her shoulders, and her dark eyes carried the weight of secrets older than the manor itself.

His grandmother, Seraphina Vale.

"You followed her," she said.

"I escorted her to the café."

"You gave her your coat."

"She was cold."

"You walked beside her."

"The streets were dangerous."

Seraphina smiled.

"Oh, Adrian."

He hated that smile.

It was the smile of someone who had already seen the ending.

"You have not asked me why I am concerned," she continued.

"I assume you are about to tell me."

"The girl has awakened something in you."

Adrian's jaw tightened.

"Nothing has awakened."

"Then place your hand on your heart."

He did not move.

"Do it," she whispered.

Slowly, Adrian pressed his palm against his chest.

Silence.

Then—

Thump.

He staggered backward.

Another beat followed.

Thump.

Thump.

A sound he had not heard in two hundred and seventeen years.

His heart was beating.

Seraphina's smile vanished.

"Oh, my poor boy."

Adrian stared at his own hand.

"What is happening to me?"

The old woman crossed herself, then whispered a name in a language that had been dead for centuries.

"Elaria."

Adrian looked up.

"What does that mean?"

Seraphina's eyes filled with tears.

"It means the prophecy has begun."

Outside, thunder shook the manor.

Far away, in the Moonlight Café, Elena Rose suddenly dropped a glass.

She pressed a hand to her chest.

Her heart was racing.

She had no idea why.

She had no idea that somewhere across town, an immortal man was experiencing the same impossible sensation.

And she certainly had no idea that beneath her skin, hidden in the place where her heartbeat lived, a faint silver mark had begun to glow.

A mark that had not appeared for five hundred years.

A mark that belonged to the oldest vampire bloodline in existence.

A mark that meant one thing.

The vampire who loved her would either become human again—

Or destroy the world trying to keep her alive.

End of Chapter One.

Some hearts beat for love. Others awaken because of it.
