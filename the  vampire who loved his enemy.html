The Vampire Who Loved His Enemy

Some hearts are forbidden to beat together. Others are willing to start a war just to prove they can.

Chapter One: The Prince Without a Heart

For three hundred years, Prince Kael Veyron had been taught that humans were weak, dishonest, and unworthy of mercy. He was the last heir of the Nightborn vampires, a royal bloodline whose eyes turned silver whenever their hunger awakened.

Kael had conquered cities, defeated armies, and watched kingdoms crumble beneath the moon. Yet nothing had ever frightened him—until the night a human woman pointed a silver arrow at his heart.

Her name was Seraphina Vale, captain of the Dawn Guard, an order sworn to destroy every vampire alive.

"One movement," she warned, "and I'll send you back to the darkness you crawled out of."

Kael glanced at the arrow. "You should have aimed for my head. My heart has been dead for centuries."

"Then killing you should be easy."

"You would think so."

She released the arrow.

Kael could have dodged it. Instead, he watched it strike his chest. The enchanted silver burned through his coat, and he fell to one knee. Seraphina stared at him, surprised. Vampires did not usually allow themselves to be wounded.

Before she could fire again, the forest erupted with snarls. Creatures with antlers, black wings, and human hands crawled from the trees. The Hollowborn had arrived—the monsters even vampires feared.

Kael seized Seraphina's wrist. "Hate me later. Run now."

"I'd rather die than run with you."

"Then try to keep up with my dying body."

He pulled her behind a fallen tree just as the creatures attacked. For the first time in three centuries, the vampire prince fought beside a human.

By dawn, the Hollowborn lay scattered across the forest. Seraphina had a cut above her eyebrow. Kael's wound had not healed.

She looked at the dark veins spreading from the arrow wound. "Silver should have killed you."

"It usually does."

"Why isn't it healing?"

Kael looked into her eyes. "Because the arrow was made from the same magic that created the Hollowborn. Someone wants me dead before I discover why they're returning."

Seraphina lowered her weapon, but only slightly. "And why should I help you?"

"You shouldn't."

It was the first honest answer she had ever heard from a vampire.

Chapter Two: The Girl in His Castle

Seraphina followed Kael to his castle because the Hollowborn had taken her younger brother, and the vampire prince promised to help her find him. She told herself that trusting Kael was a temporary mistake. Her heart, unfortunately, had not received the warning.

His castle stood on a cliff above a black sea. Portraits of dead kings lined the halls, and every servant lowered their eyes when Kael passed. He was feared even in his own home.

But Seraphina discovered things that did not fit the monster in her stories. Kael fed prisoners before questioning them. He kept a library of human history. He secretly sent medicine to villages destroyed by vampire raids.

"Why do you help people who hate you?" she asked one evening.

Kael closed his book. "Because I remember what it feels like when everyone decides what you are before asking who you might become."

For a moment, Seraphina saw the lonely boy hidden beneath the ancient prince.

Days became weeks. They searched forgotten temples and questioned captured creatures. They argued over everything, from strategy to whether human tea tasted like boiled leaves. Kael insisted that it did. Seraphina threatened to pour an entire pot over his head.

Then came the night the castle lost its power. Seraphina found Kael on a balcony, staring at the moon.

"Are you afraid of dying?" she asked.

"I was afraid of living forever long before I met you."

His words left her strangely breathless.

"You don't know anything about me," she whispered.

"I know you pretend not to be frightened. I know you talk to your brother's old scarf when you think no one is listening. I know you laugh when you're angry, and I know you keep standing even when the world gives you every reason to fall."

She looked away. "Stop looking at me like that."

"Like what?"

"Like I'm something worth saving."

Kael stepped closer. "You are."

For one dangerous moment, the enemy's hand rested against the enemy's cheek. Seraphina kissed him first. The kiss was brief, frightened, and full of everything they could never tell their kingdoms.

Then a bell rang through the castle.

A messenger stumbled onto the balcony carrying a bloodstained letter. The king of the human realm had discovered their alliance. Seraphina's brother was alive—but he was being held at the capital, and the king demanded Kael's head in exchange for his freedom.

At the bottom of the letter was a second message, written in a strange black ink:

The Hollowborn were created by the first vampire. The prince's blood is the key to opening their prison forever.

Chapter Three: A Kingdom of Ashes

Kael understood the truth before Seraphina did. His father had not died protecting the kingdom. He had helped imprison the Hollowborn centuries ago, using the blood of his own family to seal the gate. Now someone intended to use Kael's death to open it.

The traitor was Lord Malrec, the royal adviser who had served the vampire throne for two hundred years. He had secretly promised the Hollowborn a world without sunlight, humans, or rival kings.

Malrec captured Seraphina and brought her to the ruined temple beneath the capital. Kael arrived alone, carrying no sword.

"You came to die," Malrec said.

"No," Kael replied. "I came to disappoint you."

The battle shook the temple. Malrec's magic tore stones from the walls, and the Hollowborn crawled through cracks in the ancient gate. Kael fought until his clothes were soaked with blood. Seraphina broke free and drove her blade through Malrec's shoulder.

But the gate continued opening. The only way to close it was for a vampire and a human to join their blood willingly, creating a bond stronger than the spell that had sealed the prison.

"If we do this," Kael said, holding Seraphina's hand, "you may lose everything you know."

"I have already lost the life I imagined."

"You could still leave."

She smiled through her tears. "And miss the pleasure of arguing with you for the rest of my life? Absolutely not."

They pressed their palms together. Their blood met, glowing gold and silver. The gate screamed as the ancient magic awakened. Every Hollowborn creature was dragged back into the darkness, and the temple began to collapse.

Kael fell.

Seraphina caught him, pressing her forehead against his. His silver eyes were fading.

"Stay with me," she begged.

"I thought you wanted me dead."

"I have changed my mind."

"A terrible habit."

She laughed and cried at the same time. Then the bond between them burst into light.

Kael's heart beat once.

Then again.

He opened his eyes, no longer entirely vampire. The bond had changed them both: Seraphina could hear the ancient magic beneath the earth, while Kael could feel warmth in his hands for the first time in centuries.

When they emerged from the ruins, human soldiers and vampire warriors surrounded them. Both armies raised their weapons.

Seraphina stood beside Kael rather than behind him.

"If you want another war," she called, "you will have to begin by walking through both of us."

Neither army moved.

Peace did not arrive that morning. Too many people had lost too much, and hatred did not disappear because two people had fallen in love. But the soldiers lowered their weapons one by one.

Years later, the kingdoms would remember the vampire prince who learned to feel again and the human captain who dared to love her enemy. Some called their union a miracle. Others called it a dangerous mistake.

Kael never cared what they called it.

Every night, when Seraphina rested beside him, he listened to the steady rhythm of his heart and remembered the woman who had once promised to kill him.

She still threatened to do so whenever he stole her blankets.

And for the first time in three hundred years, the vampire prince had something he was terrified to lose—and something worth living forever to protect.
