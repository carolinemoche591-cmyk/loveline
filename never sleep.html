In this town, dreams are deadly.

Never Sleep

He dreamed of death. Then the town began dying.

Written by Loveline

Chapter 1: The First Dream

Every night, sixteen-year-old Finn dreamed of a black bird sitting on a church bell.

In the dream, the bird always spoke one name. The following morning, the person named would be found dead.

At first, Finn believed it was coincidence. Then the bird whispered his teacher's name.

By sunrise, the police had closed the school.

Finn stopped sleeping. He drank coffee until his hands shook and watched the horizon until dawn.

But on the fourth night, exhaustion defeated him.

The bird appeared again.

This time, it spoke his mother's name.

Chapter 2: The Watcher

Finn told his best friend, June, everything. She believed him after he described a dream in which her missing brother stood beside a flooded road.

They searched the town archives and discovered that similar deaths had occurred every twenty-seven years. Each case began with reports of nightmares and ended with the church bell ringing at midnight.

In an old photograph, Finn noticed a figure standing behind the crowd. The figure wore the same dark coat he had seen in his dreams.

June enlarged the image.

"Finn," she whispered. "That's you."

Chapter 3: The Bell Tower

They climbed the church tower after midnight. The bell was silent, but black feathers covered the floor.

Finn found a diary belonging to the town's first minister. It described a creature that fed on fear and used dreams to mark its victims.

The creature could not enter the waking world unless someone rang the bell willingly.

Then June heard her brother calling from the stairwell.

She ran toward the voice. Finn caught her wrist.

"That's not him."

A shadow unfolded across the wall, taller than any human. Its face was a blank patch of darkness.

"You have been watching me," it said.

Finn's knees trembled, but he stood between it and June.

Chapter 4: The Final Dream

The creature offered Finn a bargain. If he rang the bell, it would stop the deaths and return June's brother. If he refused, the nightmares would continue until the entire town was gone.

Finn looked at the bell rope. He thought of his mother, sleeping at home, unaware of the danger.

Then he remembered the diary's final line: Fear gives the creature a voice. Choice takes it away.

Finn refused to ring the bell.

Instead, he spoke the names of everyone who had died. With every name, the shadows grew thinner. June joined him, reading from the diary, until the creature began to scream.

The bell rang once by itself.

The creature shattered into a storm of black feathers that vanished through the windows.

At dawn, Finn returned home and found his mother alive. The town was quiet, and for the first time in weeks, he felt tired without being afraid.

That night, he slept.

In his dream, the black bird sat on a distant rooftop. It watched him silently, then spread its wings and flew away.

Finn never saw it again.
