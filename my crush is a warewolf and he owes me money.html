My Crush Is a Werewolf, and He Owes Me Money

Falling for the mysterious guy in class was bad enough. Discovering he turned into a giant wolf every full moon was worse. Discovering he had borrowed money from her and forgotten to repay it was unforgivable.

Chapter One: The Handsome Liar

Jade had two weaknesses: handsome boys and online shopping. Unfortunately, both were expensive.

Her newest weakness was Lucas, the quiet student who sat at the back of her literature class. He had dark hair, beautiful eyes, and the habit of looking at Jade as though she were the only person in the room.

He also had terrible time management.

"Can I borrow two hundred?" he asked one afternoon. "I'll pay you tomorrow."

Jade narrowed her eyes. "Are you sure? Because my bank account is currently surviving on hope."

"I promise."

She sent him the money.

Three days passed. Then a week. Then two weeks.

Every time she asked, Lucas gave her a new excuse.

"My banking app is broken."

"My uncle is visiting."

"The moon is affecting my finances."

Jade stared at him. "What does the moon have to do with your debt?"

"Nothing. I panicked."

She should have known something was wrong when Lucas vanished every full moon. He never attended evening study sessions, avoided the forest near campus, and refused to explain why he carried a packet of raw meat in his backpack.

One Friday night, Jade followed him into the woods. She expected to catch him meeting another girl or selling stolen textbooks.

Instead, she watched him transform into a massive grey wolf.

His clothes fell into a heap. His eyes glowed gold. He looked directly at her.

Jade stared back.

"You," she whispered, "owe me two hundred shillings."

The wolf blinked.

"Don't you dare pretend you don't understand me."

The wolf lowered its head.

"And another thing! If you can transform into an animal, you can definitely find a job!"

The wolf sneezed.

Jade went home and wrote three things in her diary: Lucas was a werewolf, she was apparently in love with an idiot, and she was increasing the interest on his debt.

Chapter Two: Full Moon Problems

The following Monday, Lucas cornered Jade outside the library.

"You saw me," he said.

"No, I saw a very large dog with financial problems."

"Jade, please."

She crossed her arms. "Are you going to eat me?"

"No!"

"Good. Because I'm not delicious, and I have plans this weekend."

Lucas explained that his family had been werewolves for generations. Most could control their transformations, but his abilities were stronger than usual, and he had been afraid Jade would see him as a monster.

"I don't think you're a monster," she said. "I think you're a terrible borrower."

He laughed so hard that a passing student turned around.

They started spending time together. Lucas helped Jade study, and Jade helped Lucas practise telling the truth without adding unnecessary drama. She learned that he loved old books, hated thunderstorms, and was secretly afraid of hurting someone when he transformed.

One evening, he walked her home beneath a bright moon.

"Why do you like me?" he asked.

"You want the honest answer or the romantic one?"

"Both."

"The honest answer is that you're kind when you forget to be mysterious, and you make me laugh. The romantic answer is that your eyes are unfair and your smile should probably be regulated by law."

Lucas looked at her as if she had given him the world.

"I like you too."

"Good. Now pay me."

He transferred the money immediately.

Jade checked her phone. "Interest is still outstanding."

Before Lucas could answer, a howl rose from the forest. His smile disappeared. Another wolf was calling him—one that did not belong to his family.

He gripped Jade's hand.

"You need to stay away from the woods tonight."

"Why?"

"Because something has been hunting young werewolves. And I think it followed me here."

Chapter Three: The Wolf Who Chose Love

The next full moon, Lucas disappeared before sunset. Jade was determined not to follow him. She made tea, watched a film, and reminded herself that she was a responsible person who did not chase supernatural creatures into forests.

Then his sister called.

Lucas had gone to protect a younger werewolf from a hunter who used enchanted silver traps. He had not returned.

Jade grabbed her torch and ran into the woods.

She found Lucas wounded beside an old stone wall. A silver trap had caught his leg, and a hunter stood nearby preparing another weapon.

Jade did the only thing she could think of.

She screamed, "HEY! YOU OWE ME MONEY!"

The hunter turned in confusion. Lucas, even while injured, looked offended.

"Now?" he managed to say.

"Especially now!"

She threw a rock at the hunter's torch, plunging the path into darkness. Lucas broke free and charged. The hunter fled, leaving behind the silver traps.

By dawn, Lucas was human again, exhausted and leaning against Jade's shoulder.

"You came for me," he whispered.

"Of course I did."

"You could have been hurt."

"And you could have been dead. I prefer my boyfriend alive."

He lifted his head. "Boyfriend?"

Jade blinked. "Did you want to be my enemy? Because I have a whole list of reasons."

Lucas smiled. "Boyfriend sounds better."

Months later, Jade had met his family, learned which forests to avoid, and become the only human allowed to put a tiny red scarf around Lucas's neck after a transformation.

He still occasionally forgot things. He once turned up at her house with a chicken in his arms because he had mistaken a grocery list for a werewolf training schedule.

Jade laughed so hard she had to sit down.

"I love you," Lucas said, looking embarrassed.

"I love you too."

"Even though I'm a werewolf?"

"Even though you're a werewolf."

"And I borrowed money from you?"

Jade slowly turned toward him.

"Don't ruin the moment."

He kissed her, laughing against her lips.

Jade had wanted a handsome boyfriend. She had not expected claws, moonlit adventures, or a supernatural family group chat.

But she had found someone who made her feel brave, who told her the truth, and who always came back to apologise when he got things wrong.

She loved him with her whole heart.

She also kept a written record of every loan.

Love was magical. Trust was important. And nobody—not even a werewolf—was getting away with owing Jade money.
